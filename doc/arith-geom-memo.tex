\textbf{Audit revision (22 October 2026).}
The algebraic-group viewpoint gives useful exact identities and containing
support bounds, but it does not turn N1/A10 into impossibility theorems.
Necessary smoothness requirements use section-specific support hypotheses.
The full development and Popovych note have been corrected accordingly;
the tracked PDFs are historical and do not contain these revisions.
Current status is in \texttt{doc/integration-review-status.md}.

\section*{1. The dictionary}

The AKS congruence $(X-1)^n \equiv X^n - 1 \pmod{n, X^r-1}$ is a
\emph{Frobenius-lift test}: $\Z[X]/(X^r{-}1)$ is the $\Lambda$-ring of
$\mu_r$ with Adams operations $\psi^k(X)=X^k$. Prime $n$ implies
$\psi^n(f)\equiv f^n\pmod n$, but the converse fails in a fixed truncated
ring: $399001=31\cdot61\cdot211$, $r=5$, passes the coincidence on every
section, inside the exception clause. Wilkerson's criterion does not assert
that converse. AKS checks one section. The exception clause
$n^2 \equiv 1 \pmod r$ says exactly that the fake Frobenius $\sigma_n$ lies
in $\mathrm{Gal}(\Q(\zeta_r)/\Q(\zeta_r)^+) = \{1, \text{conj}\}$ --- the
subgroup $\{1,\text{conj}\}$. Choosing a comparable exception for an elliptic
test is a modeling decision, not forced by this dictionary.

On the counterexample side, the Lenstra--Pomerance conditions
$p - 1 \mid n - 1$, $p + 1 \mid n + 1$ say that $n$ \emph{impersonates the
Frobenius of $p$ on both one-dimensional algebraic tori over $\F_p$} (split:
order $p-1$; nonsplit norm-one: order $p+1$). Twin-smoothness of
$(\frac{p-1}{2}, \frac{p+1}{2})$ is torus-order smoothness --- the same
condition behind the B-SIDH/SQIsign parameter hunts, which is why the
project's reliance on the Costello--Naehrig corpora was never a coincidence.

\section*{2. The five results}

\textbf{(AG2) Exact support bound; conditional smoothness model.}
For $d=\ord_r(p)$, the unique involution of $(\Z/r)^\times$
being $-1$ forces the exact \emph{inversion collapse}
$(X-1)^{p^{d/2}-1} = -X^{-1}$ (even $d$), so the control order
$\rho_r(p)$ lives on cyclotomic levels $e \mid d'$, $d' = d/2$ or $d$, and
the containing torus has dimension
$\sum_{e \mid d'} \varphi(e) = d'$. Validity ($\ord_r(n) > 2$) forces
$d \ge 3$, so \textbf{the containing model has dimension $\ge2$, with the
minimum $2$ attained exactly at $d = 4$, levels $\{1,2\}$: the double
(twin-smooth) condition} --- realizable iff $4 \mid r - 1$, and V\'a\v{n}a's
$\rho_5(p) \mid 10(p^2{-}1)$ is the special case. The burden formula
reproduces the A11 empirical table at all 11 moduli (value and minimizing
$d$) in the historical table. Converting the bound into a necessary
$B$-smoothness condition requires every prime above $B$ in the containing
product to occur in the actual order. Neither the density of failures nor
the cost of targeting them is established. No modulus direction is closed.

\textbf{(AG5) Conditional comparison for Popovych's pair.}
The first section has a torsion cofactor under inversion.
The second Popovych section $X+2$ has trivial global torsion-ratio
stabilizer: $(\zeta^\gamma{+}2)/(\zeta{+}2)$ is not even a unit of
$\Z[\zeta_r]$ for $\gamma \ne 1$ (disjoint ideal supports: norms $r$ vs
$(2^r{+}1)/3$). This rules out a global torsion-cofactor identity, not
special reductions: at $(r,p)=(13,5)$, $(X+2)^{24}\in\mu_{26}$.
With a separate support-sharpness hypothesis the pair has model dimension
burden $\min\{d : d \mid r{-}1, d \ge 3\} \ge 3$, equal to $4$ at Popovych's
own $r = 5$: pool primes would need $p-1$, $p+1$ \emph{and} $p^2+1$
simultaneously smooth. At the frozen harvest spec (pool primes
${\approx}51$ bits) that extra ${\approx}102$-bit level is a Dickman tax
$\rho(102/\log_2 B)$: $3.9\times10^{-11}$ over the solver base $B = 1259$,
still $1.8\times10^{-7}$ over the generous $B' = 12590$ in the
polynomial-smoothness heuristic, not a universal harvest bound.
Bonus structure: the second congruence's
$X{=}1$ component is exactly the base-3 Fermat test $3^n \equiv 3$, and
mod $3$ the two sections coincide.

\textbf{(AG5b, Oct 22 addendum) Does the ``2'' matter? No --- but ``not
$1$'' is subtly incomplete.} For any integer $c \notin \{0, \pm 1\}$ the
global argument applies to $X+c$ with the stated exclusions; a necessary
smoothness jump additionally needs that section's support hypothesis,
with $c$ entering through the norm
$\Phi_r(-c) = (c^r{+}1)/(c{+}1)$ (finitely many excluded $p$, with
$p \mid c(c{+}1)$), the excluded modulus $r \mid c{+}1$, and the level-one
component --- the base-$(c{+}1)$ Fermat test. What must be avoided is
exactly degeneracy of the section's zero $-c$: \emph{it must be a point of
$\mathbb{G}_m$ avoiding the rational torsion $\{\pm 1\}$}, equivalently
$c \notin \{0, \pm 1\}$. Here $c = 0$ is excluded trivially, not subtly:
$X$ is a monomial, $(X)^n \equiv X^n$ is an identity in every ring (the
zero sits at the origin, off the torus, and the section is a unit of
finite order $r$). Among nonzero $c$: $c = -1$ is the first section
itself, and the innocent-looking $c = +1$
fails \emph{outright}. Its zero $-1$ is inversion-fixed, so the collapse
survives ($\ord(X{+}1) \mid r(p^{d/2}{-}1)$ at even $d$, measured
$22/22$); worse, $\zeta{+}1 = (\zeta^2{-}1)/(\zeta{-}1)$ with both
numerator and denominator values of $X - 1$ at primitive roots, whose
orders divide $\rho_r(p)$ --- so the mechanism congruences plus the
Carmichael condition imply $T(+1, n, r)$ \emph{automatically} (proved:
the $X+1$ proposition in the note and full document; historical checks: $48/48$
synthetic mechanism instances pass $T(+1)$, $0/48$ pass $T(+2)$). The
hypothetical ``$\{X{-}1, X{+}1\}$ conjecture'' --- Popovych's statement
with $2$ replaced by $1$ --- is therefore \emph{mechanism-equivalent} to
Agrawal's: formally stronger-looking, but every counterexample the known
engine could ever build defeats both simultaneously. In stabilizer terms
$U(X \pm 1) = \{\pm 1\}$ while $U(X + c) = \{1\}$ otherwise: $c = 2$ is
simply the smallest positive nondegenerate constant in this comparison ($c = -2$
works equally, with Mersenne norm $2^r - 1$).

\textbf{(AG3) The impersonation hierarchy --- our targets have a name, and
the wall has a shape.} Define cells by (level set, Frobenius powers); at
level 2 the power's parity is a sign. Then: Carmichael $= (\{1\}, 0)$,
infinite (AGP); Lucas--Carmichael $= (\{2\}, 1)$, infinite (Wright 2018);
the L--P targets $= (\{1,2\}, (0,1))$ are the \emph{Williams numbers}
(Williams 1977, Echi) --- open even for nonemptiness, none below $2^{64}$
(McIntosh--Mitra 2015). Yet Howe's \emph{rigid order-2 Carmichael number}
$443{,}372{,}888{,}629{,}441 = 17{\cdot}31{\cdot}41{\cdot}43{\cdot}89
{\cdot}97{\cdot}167{\cdot}331$ occupies the \emph{same level set} with
all-even parities and exists at $4.4 \times 10^{14}$ (re-verified).
\textbf{Sign splitting is a useful distinction, not a proved wall}: rigid cells are one-sided
AGP problems (single target $1$); the Williams parities $(0,1)$ force
targets $(+1, -1)$ that conflict on shared prime powers, forcing the
coprime side-split studied by N1/A10. Those finite experiments do not prove
unconstructibility. The original full-bound $r=5$ design requires an odd
matching exponent; sharp datum: Howe's $n \equiv 1 \pmod 5$ does not
even pass $T(-1,n,5)$ --- rigidity misses the $\mu_{2r}$ torsion the
mod-80 conditions exist to fix.

\textbf{(AG4) A stabilizer-index upper bound, not elliptic closure.}
The chosen commuting endomorphism-lift formulation restricts to CM curves;
it does not exclude all other elliptic tests. Gurney's thesis treats CM
moduli and curves of Shimura type with the relevant base hypotheses.
On an ordinary split eigenline Frobenius
acts on an $r$-eigenline by a scalar $\lambda$, curve automorphisms give
exact collapses (e.g.\ $x([i]S) = -x(S)$ on $y^2 = x^3 - x$), and
everything unifies:
\[
\ord(f(T))\mid L(p^{k'}-1),\qquad
k'=[\langle\lambda\rangle:\langle\lambda\rangle\cap U],
\]
$L$ a bound on the cofactor orders, and $U$ the cofactor subgroup
($\{\pm1\}$ for $X{-}1$ $\to$ AG2;
$\{1\}$ for $X{+}2$ $\to$ AG5; $\mu_4/\mu_6$ for CM $x$-coordinates). The
tantalizing index-$1$ cells ($\lambda\equiv\pm i$) lie in the chosen
exception subgroup. Index-$2$ cells have a twin-smooth \emph{containing}
support; necessity would require the extra saturation hypothesis.
The historical computations on
$y^2=x^3-x$ reported model floors $3$ ($r=13$) and $2$ ($r=17$),
matching the index formula; elliptic $\ge$ torus model dimension at all 11 listed moduli.
These do not prove support saturation, the necessity of the chosen exception,
or transfer of a solver obstruction. The elliptic direction remains open.

\section*{3. Epistemic status}

Exact: the torus inversion identity, its order upper bound, the
characteristic-zero non-unit argument, the stated $X+1$ implication, and
the cofactor-index upper bound under its explicit assumptions.

Conditional: necessary smoothness requirements, assuming prime support at
the chosen threshold for each section. A cyclic-group calculation uses
$\ell^{v_\ell(N)}$-th powers, not just $\ell$-th powers; algebraic dependencies
prevent assuming independent uniform reductions without proof.

Unestablished: a density theorem for exceptional orders, production-scale
harvest/solver costs, universal elliptic closure, and the proposed
function-field sharpness and smooth-order counting theorems. Weil's bounds
for specified extensions do not establish those counting statements without
extensions, asymptotic regimes and uniform error estimates.
Historical committed JSON files are finite checks, not proofs of these claims.

\section*{4. Where this leaves the program}

\begin{itemize}\itemsep1pt
\item \textbf{Historical negative-result paper}: remains on the separate
\texttt{negative-result-writeup} branch. It is not merged or revised by this
audit. Its theorem and infeasibility claims require independent correction.
\item \textbf{Standalone Popovych note}: the current source distinguishes

cost. It is still a working note, not certified ready for publication.
\item \textbf{Constructive work}: validate the real pool's units, prime-power
moduli, target reachability, parity and component distribution before scaling.
A8/A10 forced-zero parameters are not A12 literal identity probabilities;
the quoted ${\sim}0.004$ is not a verified full H7 component-density measure.
No single surviving solver paradigm has been proved to be the only option.
\item \textbf{Open problems surfaced}: unconditional sharpness
(equidistribution); existence of Williams numbers at all (independent
interest; the sign-split lens suggests why the AGP machine hasn't reached
them); whether any side-separating polynomial family reaches an admissible
local cell (old Q6.2, still open).
\end{itemize}
