## 1. Split-relation saturation lemma

Let `G` be a finite direct sum of cyclic prime-power groups and let `H` be
generated by the declared vectors. Suppose coordinates `a` and `b` are
**both cyclic of order two**, and every generator has equal entries there.
Let `K = {g in G : g[a] = g[b]}`.

Then `K` is a split subgroup of index two: drop coordinate `b` and recover
it from `a`. Assume:

1. In the 2-primary part, the generator matrix after dropping `b` has full
   row rank modulo two.
2. For every odd primary part, the generator matrix has full row rank
   modulo that prime.

**Conclusion:** `H = K`, hence `[G:H] = 2`, and a target is an integer
subgroup member exactly when its two named coordinates agree.

**Proof.** Each primary projection of `H` is generated by the corresponding
projected vectors; finite abelian subgroups split over primary parts.
Full rank for prime `q` says `H_q + q*K_q = K_q` (with `K_q = G_q` for
odd `q`). Thus the finite `q`-group `Q = K_q/H_q` satisfies `Q = q*Q`.
Iterating until a power of `q` kills `Q` gives `Q = 0`. So each primary
projection equals `K_q`, proving the conclusion.

The order-two and split hypotheses are essential. A diagonal subgroup in
`C4 x C4` also has a one-dimensional binary rank deficit, but has index
four; that deficit alone does not establish this lemma. Failure of a
hypothesis means **not certified by this criterion**, not nonmembership.

The checker independently verifies the split relation and a full-rank
minor for **every original primary part**. Certificates bind each prime,
coordinate set and selected generator index; missing, repeated or swapped
primary parts are invalid. The checker does not call certificate generation
or a subgroup-membership solver.

## 2. One exact preprocessing configuration

For an exact odd support `k`, use one bit per even-order original component,
plus the required odd-cardinality bit. A generator's bit column is its
coordinate parity; the target has the corresponding parity syndrome.
These are necessary equations, not independent bit slices of higher powers.

Split `k = floor(k/2) + ceil(k/2)`. Store every left combination by its exact
binary syndrome. Enumerate right combinations, look up the complementary
syndrome, and require **disjoint** index sets. Preserve all collisions.
For every match, recompute the sum in **all original component orders**.
Only a subset passing that check and the independent original-prime residue
check can count as a solution.

XOR is used only for the valid binary projection of original columns.
No additive residual is assumed for overlapping kernel moves, no modular
carry is discarded, and no planting witness reaches the worker.

This moves the expensive full-group work behind a cheap necessary filter.
It is exact-cardinality low-support recovery, **not general odd-subset
search** and not a scalable solution at support 93 or 191.

## 5. Interpretation and next boundary

The arithmetic gate is complete for these two exact frozen instances:
index two and integer membership follow without a longer subgroup search.
The Boolean solver milestone is narrower: **this exact-k configuration
recovered all six declared support-5 controls**, including the untouched
holdouts. Six successes do not establish a statistical reliability guarantee.

The gain comes from screening tiny-support combinations in a strong binary
projection before evaluating their large full-group residuals. It is not a
CP-SAT improvement, a general odd-subset solver, or evidence that the real
target is near. The 93- and 191-factor lower bounds remain unchanged.
The current combination/table guards exclude brute-force extensions to the
full-483 support-9/17 cases; they were not run.

The next separately frozen investigation should address general-odd recovery
or a principled higher-support method, with independent controls and exact
residual handling. Do not extrapolate this low-support success to the real
targets or expand budgets automatically.
