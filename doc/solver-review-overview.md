## 1. The project in one paragraph

Agrawal's conjecture (from the AKS "PRIMES is in P" work) would, if true, give a
Õ(log³n) deterministic primality test; it is believed false. The only known
route to a counterexample is the Lenstra–Pomerance construction: build a
composite n = p₁⋯p_k, squarefree, k ≡ 1 or 3 (mod 4), where every pᵢ ≡ 3
(mod 80) and — the crux — both pᵢ−1 | n−1 and pᵢ+1 | n+1 (making n
simultaneously a Carmichael and Lucas–Carmichael number, with side conditions).
No such n is known to exist; heuristics say they are abundant but astronomically
large. This repo's program: instead of *searching* for one, *construct* one, via
a two-stage pipeline — **harvest** a pool of eligible primes p = 2m+1 with
(m, m+1) twin-smooth over disjoint prime supports, then **solve** a
subset-product problem (find a subset whose product hits the right residues
mod Λ₋ = lcm(p−1) and Λ₊ = lcm(p+1)). The reduction is proved as Prop 3.1 in
`doc/working-paper.tex`.

## 2. The measured arc (all numbers in `data/FINDINGS.md`, dated sections)

1. **Phase 0/1a (Aug 6):** machinery validated; discovered the **0.06
   invariant** — mining any fixed twin-smooth corpus, the best side-partition
   keeps only ~0.06 usable elements per demand-bit (needs ≳1). Measured seven
   ways, up to the complete 82M-pair B=547 corpus. Dataset mining: dead.
2. **A1/A2 (Aug 7):** the **partition-first harvester** (fix the prime
   partition, then enumerate) bypasses that invariant: measured pool/demand
   ratios 13.8–44×. Gates G1, G2 passed; supply is a solved problem.
3. **A4 (Aug 7):** first solver measurement. The condition linearizes to 0/1
   subset-sum in G = (Z/Λ₋)*×(Z/Λ₊)* (~18k bits, thousands of cyclic
   components). The mod-2 part solves by GF(2) elimination at full scale — but
   is only ~16% of the bits. The odd part is the real problem.
4. **A5/A6 (Aug 7–8):** co-designing the harvest shrinks the odd *primes*
   (≤97) but not the odd *dimension*; generic solvers (exact MITM, annealing)
   cap 2–3 orders of magnitude below what's needed. Gate G3: failed.
5. **A7/A8 (Aug 8):** identified the one method that has solved
   subset-products at this scale — AGHS (arXiv:1203.6664), who built a
   Carmichael number with 10¹⁰ prime factors via an ω-guided reduction. But it
   requires pools whose vectors are identity on most components (**high
   identity-density**, from the "p−1 divides a fixed Λ" structure).
6. **N1 (Aug 8):** the harvest paradigm that would give that density (H8:
   fix Λ₋, Λ₊, harvest p with p−1 | Λ₋ AND p+1 | Λ₊) came up **empty (0–2
   primes) at every scale tested** (factor bases up to ~68 primes).
7. **A10 (Aug 8):** measured the density law for birthday-class solvers:
   reach ≈ O(log N)/(1−ρ). Our harvestable pools have ρ ≈ 0.004; the solver
   needs ρ ≳ 0.98. **Option (a) closed** — low-density pools are unsolvable,
   high-density pools unharvestable.
8. **A11 (Aug 24):** no other AKS modulus r escapes: every valid r yields the
   same double p±1 condition or worse. **Option (c) closed.**

State entering this session: the route is blocked at the solver by a quantified
two-sided obstruction; the live options were **(b)** find a fundamentally
different solver paradigm, and **(d)** write up the negative result.

## 3. What this session did

Task: step back and survey the literature for solver directions, with honest
assessments. Output: `doc/solver-directions-review.md` (assessments and
recommendations) and `doc/solver-directions-review-details.md` (derivations,
provenance, and verification status for every claim — including which sources
could only be read at abstract/snippet level because this environment's proxy
blocks PDF downloads). Three results, in decreasing order of confidence:

**(i) The wall matches proven conditional hardness (high confidence in the
literature, explicitly an analogy for our instance).** Our regime — one dense
±/0-1 combination of N items forced to hit a target far more precisely than
2^-polylog(N) — is the regime of the random Number Partitioning Problem, where
Gamarnik–Kızıldağ proved an Overlap Gap Property (a barrier for stable
algorithms — our failed annealing is the textbook case) and
Vafa–Vaikuntanathan (STOC 2025) proved that doing better than the
Karmarkar–Karp bound is as hard as worst-case lattice problems. Our demand
(~18,000 bits) is ~3× beyond even their hardness threshold (log³N ≈ 5,800
bits), let alone the algorithmic bound (log²N ≈ 320). **This is not a theorem
about our instance** — the details doc enumerates the five modeling gaps — but
it says the generic version of what we need is believed impossible by the
lattice-crypto community, which reframes option (b): only instance structure
could save it, and the survey found no handle. It also upgrades the negative
paper from "our solvers failed" to "the regime is conditionally hard."

**(ii) Our wall has an independent 20-year-old twin (well-sourced facts,
consequential inference flagged).** Chen–Greene (Fibonacci Quart. 2003),
attacking the Pomerance–Selfridge–Wagstaff $620 challenge (a composite passing
a Fermat test *and* a Lucas-type test — structurally our double p−1/p+1
condition), constructed a pool of **1,248 primes** into fixed large moduli,
with ~740 expected counterexamples among the 2^1248 subset products — and
stopped there, because finding one is exactly our subset-product problem
(pool ≈ demand bits ≈ 1,240). Two decades and a standing prize later, nobody
has crossed that step. This both corroborates our wall externally **and**
exposes a gap in our own evidence: N1 declared the high-density paradigm
"unharvestable" from experiments at ≤68-prime scale, while Chen–Greene shows
the analogous pool is *nonempty* (1,248) at ~1,240-bit scale, because divisor
counts grow exponentially while demand grows only linearly. Caveat, spelled
out in the details doc: their eligibility conditions (order-divisibility) are
weaker than our strict p∓1-divisibility, so their count is an optimistic bound
for us, not a transfer.

**(iii) A concrete, cheap, decisive next experiment (proposal).** **A12:**
measure the achievable (pool, demand, density) frontier of the double-condition
harvest as the fixed moduli grow highly composite — pure divisor enumeration
plus primality testing, no new solver, C-level compute we already have
(`chm_closure.c` skill set). If some design over-provisions (pool ≥ 2.5×
demand bits) at AGHS-like density, the construction reopens (then, and only
then, build the full AGHS solver — the a8 stub never reproduced it). If not,
N1 upgrades from "empty at toy scale" to a measured scaling law, and the
negative paper gains the PSW-challenge connection. My subjective prior that it
reopens the route: ~20–30% (reasoning in the details doc, §8.6); the
experiment is worth running in either direction.

Secondary findings: the infinitude-proof literature (AGP through Larsen–Wright
2025) has only ever handled *one-sided* conditions — the simultaneous double
condition is a recognized open frontier, with no importable machinery; all
other solver families surveyed (dense/modular subset-sum, zero-sum theory,
k-list refinements, lattices, SAT/ILP, quantum) are assessed and closed with
quantified reasons, except one cheap hard-core-dimension falsification run.

## 4. What is claim vs. evidence

Every claim in the review carries a provenance tier in the details doc:
**A** = measured in this repo (FINDINGS.md); **B** = established literature,
full statement verifiable; **C** = my derivation from sourced numbers
(arithmetic shown); **D** = taken from abstracts/secondary sources only —
flagged ⚠ with what a full-text read must confirm (the environment could not
fetch PDFs; the ⚠ list is: Chen–Greene 2003, Grantham's Perrin proof,
Hegde–Devaraj 2021, and the precise theorem statements of Vafa–Vaikuntanathan
and Imran–Ivanyos). Nothing in FINDINGS.md, PLAN.md, or the registry was
modified; the recommendations are proposals for the user to accept or reject.
