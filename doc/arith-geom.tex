\begin{abstract}
We reframe Agrawal's conjecture and the Lenstra--Pomerance counterexample
mechanism in the language of one-dimensional algebraic groups. The main new
result (item AG2 of \texttt{doc/arith-geom-plan.md}) upgrades the empirical
finding A11 of this project (\texttt{data/FINDINGS.md}) to a structural
statement: for \emph{every} AKS modulus $r$, the constructive
(Lenstra--Pomerance-style) route forces smoothness control on the $\F_p$-point
orders of a product of cyclotomic tori of total dimension at least $2$, and
the minimum $2$ is attained exactly by the split and nonsplit one-dimensional
tori --- orders $p-1$ and $p+1$ --- i.e.\ by the Carmichael + Lucas--Carmichael
double condition (equivalently, twin-smoothness). The key identity is an
inversion collapse: when $d=\ord_r(p)$ is even, the unique involution of
$(\Z/r)^\times$ is $-1$, forcing $(X-1)^{p^{d/2}-1}\equiv -X^{-1} \pmod{p,\,
\Phi_r(X)}$ and collapsing the order of $X-1$ into the cyclotomic levels
$e \mid d/2$. Since $\sum_{e \mid m}\varphi(e) = m$, the burden is $d/2$ for
even $d$ and $d$ for odd $d$; validity of a counterexample forces $d \ge 3$,
so the burden is minimized at $d = 4$ (possible iff $4 \mid r-1$), value $2$,
never $1$. All identities and the resulting burden table are verified
numerically (\texttt{code/ag2\_torus\_check.py}), reproducing A11's measured
table for all eleven moduli tested there. Throughout the document the
collapse identities, the stabilizer and independence lemmas, and the index
formulas are proved unconditionally; exactly \emph{one} Artin-type
hypothesis is used --- \emph{generic sharpness}, the attainment of the
support bounds (Proposition~\ref{prop:sharp}) --- named wherever it enters,
empirically supported ($0$ exceptions across $196$ sampled large primes),
and a theorem in the function-field mirror. The mechanism scope
(Frobenius-matching constructions, Remark~\ref{rem:scope}) is a separate,
stated restriction, not a hypothesis. Section~\ref{sec:popovych} (item
AG5) extends the count to Popovych's two-section conjecture: the second
section $X+2$ has trivial stabilizer in the Galois action --- its zero $-2$,
unlike the zero $1$ of $X-1$, is not inversion-stable --- so no collapse
exists at any depth, the burden rises to $\min\{d : d \mid r-1,\ d \ge 3\}
\ge 3$ for every modulus ($4$ at Popovych's own $r=5$: $p-1$, $p+1$ and
$p^2+1$ simultaneously smooth), and the twin-smooth regime, the only one
this project found supply-feasible, does not exist for the pair. This is
the first quantified evidence in either direction on Popovych's conjecture
beyond search ranges. Section~\ref{sec:hierarchy} (item AG3) organizes the
generalized-Carmichael literature into a hierarchy of \emph{impersonation
cells} indexed by cyclotomic levels and Frobenius-power parities: Carmichael,
Lucas--Carmichael, Williams and Howe's higher-order Carmichael numbers are
the cells $(\{1\},0)$, $(\{2\},1)$, $(\{1,2\},(0,1))$ and depth-$2$-any-power
respectively, and the striking asymmetry of the record --- rigid depth-$2$
impersonators exist at $4.4\times10^{14}$ (Howe) while the Williams cell is
empty to $2^{64}$ --- shows that \emph{sign splitting}, not depth, is the
constructible/blocked boundary, in exact agreement with this project's
N1/A10 measurements. Section~\ref{sec:elliptic} (item AG4) settles the
elliptic axis: a canonical elliptic Agrawal congruence exists only for CM
curves, and there a single \emph{stabilizer-index theorem} --- burden $=
[\langle\lambda\rangle : \langle\lambda\rangle \cap U]$, where $U$ is the
unit-stabilizer of the test section --- subsumes
Theorems~\ref{thm:dimcount} and \ref{thm:popburden} and gives the same
floor of $2$: the would-be burden-$1$ cells lie inside the exception
clause (they are the elliptic analogue of rigid Carmichael numbers), the
burden-$2$ cells demand twin-smoothness again with sign-split targets, and
gate AG-G2 is not passed --- the negative result extends to the
CM-elliptic axis. Appendix~\ref{app:ff} (item AG6) sketches the
function-field mirror, where both heuristic ingredients of the analysis
are theorems by Weil's RH for curves while the problem itself carries no
complexity content --- locating the residual difficulty over $\Z$
precisely in the unconditional-equidistribution gap.
\end{abstract}

\section{The Frobenius-lift dictionary}\label{sec:dictionary}

This section fixes the geometric reading used throughout (item AG1). Write
$T(-1,n,r)$ for the AKS congruence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:aks}
(X-1)^n \equiv X^n - 1 \pmod{n,\, X^r-1},
\end{equation}
$r$ an odd prime not dividing $n$.

\begin{proposition}[primality as a Frobenius lift]\label{prop:lift}
Let $A_n = (\Z/n)[X]/(X^r-1)$, the mod-$n$ group algebra of $\mu_r$, and let
$\sigma_n : X \mapsto X^n$ be the $n$-th Adams operation (well defined since
$X^r = 1$). If $n$ is prime, then the $n$-power map on $A_n$ coincides with
$\sigma_n$; that is, $f(X)^n \equiv f(X^n) \pmod{n,\,X^r-1}$ for
\emph{every} $f$. The congruence \eqref{eq:aks} is this coincidence tested on
the single element $f = X-1$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For $n$ prime the $n$-power map is a ring endomorphism (freshman's dream) and
fixes $\Z/n$ (Fermat), so $f(X)^n \equiv f^{(n)}(X^n) \equiv f(X^n)$, where
$f^{(n)}$ raises coefficients to the $n$-th power.
\end{proof}

Before truncation, the coincidence characterizes primality:

\begin{proposition}[the untruncated test {\cite[Lemma 2.1]{AKS}}]
\label{prop:untrunc}
Let $a$ be coprime to $n \ge 2$. Then $n$ is prime if and only if
$(X + a)^n \equiv X^n + a \pmod n$ in $(\Z/n)[X]$. (If $q^k \,\|\, n$ for a
prime $q < n$, the coefficient of $X^q$ is $\binom{n}{q} a^{n-q}
\not\equiv 0 \bmod n$.) The AKS test, and Agrawal's conjecture, concern how
much of this characterization survives the truncation from the affine line
to the finite subscheme $\mu_r$.
\end{proposition}

In $\lambda$-ring language the statement is canonical rather than ad hoc.
$\Z[X]/(X^r - 1) = \Z[\Z/r]$ carries the Adams operations
$\psi^k : X \mapsto X^k$, which pairwise commute, and each $\psi^p$ is a
\emph{Frobenius lift}: $\psi^p(f) \equiv f^p \pmod p$ for every $f$ (the
calculation of Proposition~\ref{prop:lift}). By Wilkerson's criterion
\cite{Wilkerson}, a torsion-free ring with such a commuting family of
Frobenius lifts carries a unique $\lambda$-ring structure; in Borger's
absolute geometry a $\Lambda$-structure \emph{is} descent data to $\F_1$
\cite{Borger}. In this language:
\[
\text{$n$ is prime} \iff \text{$\psi^n$ is a Frobenius lift at $n$},
\]
and the AKS congruence \eqref{eq:aks} is the lift condition tested on the
single section $X - 1$ of $\mu_r$. Agrawal's conjecture is therefore a
\emph{quantitative descent statement}: one well-chosen section certifies
the lift condition globally, up to the $\sigma_n^2 = \mathrm{id}$
exception. The \emph{pseudofields} of Lenstra--Pomerance \cite{LPGauss}
are the operational form of the same object --- finite rings equipped with
a certified Frobenius datum, which their $\tilde O(\log^6 n)$ algorithm
constructs unconditionally; an Agrawal-type conjecture asserts the datum
can be certified from a single section, at $\tilde O(\log^3 n)$.

\begin{lemma}[the exceptional clause]\label{lem:clause}
$n^2 \equiv 1 \pmod r$ iff $\sigma_n$, viewed as the element
$n \bmod r$ of $(\Z/r)^\times \cong \Gal(\Q(\zeta_r)/\Q)$, lies in
$\Gal(\Q(\zeta_r)/\Q(\zeta_r)^+) = \{1, \text{complex conjugation}\}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$(\Z/r)^\times$ is cyclic of even order $r-1$; its unique element of order
$2$ is $-1$, which acts on $\mu_r$ as inversion, i.e.\ as complex
conjugation, the generator of $\Gal(\Q(\zeta_r)/\Q(\zeta_r)^+)$.
\end{proof}

Agrawal's conjecture thus asserts: \emph{a composite Frobenius impostor on
the single section $X-1$ must act trivially on the maximal real subfield}. A
\emph{valid counterexample} is a composite $n$ with $T(-1,n,r)$ and
$t := \ord_r(n) > 2$.

\section{Setup: cyclotomic tori and the mechanism}\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Cyclotomic tori}

For $e \ge 1$ let $\Phi_e$ denote the $e$-th cyclotomic polynomial, so that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:levels}
p^d - 1 \;=\; \prod_{e \mid d} \Phi_e(p).
\end{equation}
Over $\F_p$, the Weil restriction $\Res_{\F_{p^d}/\F_p}\Gm$ decomposes up to
isogeny as $\prod_{e \mid d} T_e$, where $T_e$ is the cyclotomic torus of
dimension $\varphi(e)$ split by $\F_{p^e}$, with
$\#T_e(\F_p) = \Phi_e(p)$ \cite{Vosk,RS}. The two \emph{one-dimensional}
tori are $T_1 = \Gm$ ($\#T_1(\F_p) = p-1$, the split torus) and $T_2$ the
norm-one torus of $\F_{p^2}/\F_p$ ($\#T_2(\F_p) = p+1$, the nonsplit twist);
$\varphi(e) = 1$ iff $e \in \{1,2\}$. We refer to the index $e$ in
\eqref{eq:levels} as the \emph{level}.

\subsection{The order of $X-1$}

Fix primes $p \ne r$, $p \nmid n$, and let $d = d_p := \ord_r(p)$. Then
\[
\F_p[X]/(X^r-1) \;\cong\; \F_p \times \F_p[X]/(\Phi_r(X)), \qquad
\F_p[X]/(\Phi_r(X)) \;\cong\; \prod_{i=1}^{(r-1)/d} \F_{p^{d}},
\]
the factors indexed by the orbits of $\langle p \rangle$ on
$(\Z/r)^\times$; in the $i$-th factor $X$ maps to a primitive $r$-th root of
unity $\zeta_i$. The element $X - 1$ vanishes in the $\F_p$ factor (where
\eqref{eq:aks} holds trivially: both sides are $0$) and is a unit in
$\F_p[X]/\Phi_r$ since $\Phi_r(1) = r \ne 0$ in $\F_p$.

\begin{definition}\label{def:rho}
$\rho_r(p)$ is the multiplicative order of the image of $X-1$ in
$\bigl(\F_p[X]/\Phi_r(X)\bigr)^\times$, i.e.\
$\rho_r(p) = \lcm_i \ord(\zeta_i - 1)$.
\end{definition}

For $r = 5$ this is the quantity $\rho(p)$ of V\'a\v{n}a \cite{Vana}, with
$\rho(p) \mid 10(p^2-1)$; Corollary~\ref{cor:vana} below recovers this.

\subsection{The Lenstra--Pomerance mechanism}

\begin{definition}[L--P mechanism]\label{def:mech}
A squarefree composite $n = p_1 \cdots p_k$ coprime to $r$ is produced by the
\emph{L--P mechanism} if for every $i$ there is $j_i \ge 0$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mech}
n \;\equiv\; p_i^{\,j_i} \pmod{\lcm\bigl(r,\ \rho_r(p_i)\bigr)},
\end{equation}
and $\ord_r(n) > 2$ (validity).
\end{definition}

The original $r=5$ construction \cite{LP,Popovych09} is the instance
$j_i = 1$: the conditions $p \equiv 3 \pmod{80}$, $p-1 \mid n-1$,
$p+1 \mid n+1$ give $n \equiv p \pmod{\lcm(5,\rho_5(p))}$ once
$\rho_5(p) \mid 10(p^2-1)$ is known. Our entire Track~A pipeline
(\texttt{PLAN.md}) is a search inside Definition~\ref{def:mech}.

\begin{remark}[scope]\label{rem:scope}
The mechanism is sufficient, not necessary, for \eqref{eq:aks}: the component
equation $(\zeta-1)^n = \zeta^n - 1$ could conceivably hold ``accidentally''
for some $p$, outside the Frobenius-matching family \eqref{eq:mech}. No such
solutions are known, and no way to construct them is known; every published
counterexample construction \cite{LP,Popovych09,HegdeDevaraj} is an instance
of Definition~\ref{def:mech}. All results below quantify \emph{this
mechanism}, in line with the scope discipline of the negative-result paper
(\texttt{doc/negative-result-plan.md}).
\end{remark}

\section{The torus dimension count}\label{sec:dimcount}

\subsection{Frobenius matching and the inversion collapse}

\begin{lemma}[Frobenius matching]\label{lem:frob}
With notation as above:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] $(X-1)^{p^k} \equiv X^{p^k} - 1 \pmod{p,\, X^r - 1}$ for all
$k \ge 0$.
\item[(ii)] If $n \equiv p^{j} \pmod{\lcm(r, \rho_r(p))}$ then
$T(-1,n,r)$ holds modulo $p$.
\item[(iii)] Conversely, \eqref{eq:mech} forces $n \equiv p^{j}
\pmod r$, hence $\ord_r(n) \mid d_p$. In particular a valid counterexample
($\ord_r(n) > 2$) forces $d_p \ge 3$ for every $p \mid n$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(i) is the iterated Frobenius endomorphism of $\F_p$-algebras. (ii): write
$n = p^j + c\cdot\lcm(r,\rho_r(p))$. In the $\F_p$ factor both sides of
\eqref{eq:aks} vanish. In $\F_p[X]/\Phi_r$:
$(X-1)^n = (X-1)^{p^j}\bigl((X-1)^{\rho_r(p)}\bigr)^{c'} = (X-1)^{p^j}
\equiv X^{p^j} - 1$ by (i), and $X^{p^j} = X^{n \bmod r} = X^n$ since
$X^r \equiv 1 \bmod \Phi_r$ (as $X^r - 1 = (X-1)\Phi_r(X)$ for $r$ prime).
(iii): reduce \eqref{eq:mech} modulo $r$; then $n \in \langle p \rangle
\subseteq (\Z/r)^\times$, so $\ord_r(n)$ divides
$\#\langle p\rangle = d_p$, and $\ord_r(n) \ge 3$ gives $d_p \ge 3$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[inversion collapse]\label{lem:collapse}
Suppose $d = \ord_r(p)$ is even. Then, in $\F_p[X]/\Phi_r(X)$,
\[
(X-1)^{p^{d/2}-1} \;=\; -X^{-1},
\]
and consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:collapse}
\rho_r(p) \;\Bigm|\; 2r\,(p^{d/2}-1) \;=\; 2r \prod_{e \mid d/2} \Phi_e(p).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$(\Z/r)^\times$ is cyclic, so its unique element of order $2$ is $-1$; since
$\ord_r(p) = d$, the element $p^{d/2}$ has order $2$, whence
$p^{d/2} \equiv -1 \pmod r$. In each field component, with $X \mapsto
\zeta$ a primitive $r$-th root of unity, iterated Frobenius gives
\[
(\zeta - 1)^{p^{d/2}} = \zeta^{p^{d/2}} - 1 = \zeta^{-1} - 1
= -\zeta^{-1}(\zeta - 1),
\]
and dividing by $\zeta - 1 \ne 0$ yields $(\zeta-1)^{p^{d/2}-1} =
-\zeta^{-1}$. This holds in every component, hence in the product ring.
Finally $(-X^{-1})^{2r} = X^{-2r} = 1$, so
$(X-1)^{2r(p^{d/2}-1)} = 1$, giving \eqref{eq:collapse}; the product form is
\eqref{eq:levels} applied to $p^{d/2}-1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[V\'a\v{n}a's bound]\label{cor:vana}
For $r = 5$ and $p \equiv \pm 2 \pmod 5$ (so $d = 4$),
$\rho_5(p) \mid 10(p^2 - 1)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{definition}[support level]\label{def:support}
Set
\[
d' \;=\; d'(p) \;:=\;
\begin{cases}
d/2, & d \text{ even},\\
d, & d \text{ odd}.
\end{cases}
\]
\end{definition}

For odd $d$ the trivial bound $\rho_r(p) \mid p^d - 1 = \prod_{e\mid d}
\Phi_e(p)$ applies (orders in a product of copies of $\F_{p^d}^\times$), so
in both cases
\begin{equation}\label{eq:support}
\rho_r(p) \;\Bigm|\; 2r \prod_{e \mid d'} \Phi_e(p):
\end{equation}
\emph{the $p$-dependent part of $\rho_r(p)$ is supported on the cyclotomic
levels $e \mid d'$.}

\subsection{Generic sharpness}

The bound \eqref{eq:support} is an upper bound on support; the mechanism's
cost analysis also needs it to be generically \emph{attained} --- otherwise a
harvest could fish for primes $p$ where $\rho_r(p)$ collapses further.

\begin{proposition}[generic sharpness; heuristic + empirical]
\label{prop:sharp}
Let $\ell > 2rd$ be a prime with $\ell \mid \Phi_e(p)$ for some
$e \mid d'$. Then $\ell \mid \rho_r(p)$ unless in every component the image
of $X - 1$ is an $\ell$-th power in $\F_{p^d}^\times$. Modelling the
components as independent uniform elements, the exceptional probability is
$\ell^{-(r-1)/d}\le 1/\ell$. Consequently, for all $p$ outside a set of
(heuristic) density $O(\sum_\ell 1/\ell)$, the large-prime part of
$\prod_{e \mid d'}\Phi_e(p)$ divides $\rho_r(p)$, and requiring
``$\rho_r(p)$ is $B$-smooth'' is equivalent to requiring
``$\Phi_e(p)$ is $B$-smooth for every $e \mid d'$''.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Evidence]
$\ell \nmid \rho_r(p)$ iff the image of $X-1$ has trivial $\ell$-part in
each cyclic component group $\F_{p^d}^\times$, i.e.\ is an $\ell$-th power
in every component whose order $\ell$ divides; the $\ell$-th powers have index $\ell$ in each
component containing $\ell$-torsion. Rigorously controlling the image of
$\zeta - 1$ is an open Artin-type problem; empirically
(\texttt{code/ag2\_torus\_check.py}, 67 sample primes across
$r \in \{5,7,11,13,19,23,41\}$, $d \in \{3,4,5,6,11\}$): all $37$ tested
large primes $\ell$ divided $\rho_r(p)$ ($0$ exceptions; $\approx 0.19$
expected under the $1/\ell$ model), and in all $67$ cases removing the top
level $e = d'$ from the bound \eqref{eq:support} broke it (the top level is
genuinely present in $\rho_r(p)$).
\end{proof}

\subsection{The dimension-count theorem}

\begin{theorem}[torus dimension count]\label{thm:dimcount}
Let $r$ be an odd prime and let $n$ be produced by the L--P mechanism
(Definition~\ref{def:mech}) with $\ord_r(n) > 2$. For each $p \mid n$ put
$d_p = \ord_r(p)$ and $d_p'$ as in Definition~\ref{def:support}. Assume
generic sharpness (Proposition~\ref{prop:sharp}) at $p$. Then, for the CRT
system \eqref{eq:mech} to admit assembly over many primes with a common
$B$-smooth modulus (the setting of the Track-A pipeline), each $p \mid n$
must satisfy:
\[
\Phi_e(p) \ \text{is $B$-smooth for every } e \mid d_p'
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\#\Bigl(\textstyle\prod_{e \mid d_p'} T_e\Bigr)(\F_p) \ \text{is $B$-smooth},
\]
a smoothness condition on the $\F_p$-points of a torus of dimension
\[
\dim \prod_{e \mid d_p'} T_e \;=\; \sum_{e \mid d_p'} \varphi(e) \;=\; d_p'
\;=\;
\begin{cases}
d_p/2, & d_p \text{ even},\\
d_p, & d_p \text{ odd}.
\end{cases}
\]
Moreover:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] $d_p' \ge 2$ for every $p \mid n$: the burden is never a single
one-dimensional torus. (Indeed $d_p' = 1$ would force $d_p \in \{1,2\}$,
contradicting $d_p \ge 3$ from Lemma~\ref{lem:frob}(iii).)
\item[(b)] $d_p' = 2$ iff $d_p = 4$, in which case the level set is
$\{1,2\}$ and the condition is: $p - 1 = \#T_1(\F_p)$ and
$p + 1 = \#T_2(\F_p)$ both $B$-smooth --- the Carmichael +
Lucas--Carmichael \emph{double condition}, i.e.\ twin-smoothness of
$\bigl(\tfrac{p-1}{2}, \tfrac{p+1}{2}\bigr)$.
\item[(c)] $d_p = 4$ is realizable iff $4 \mid r - 1$; hence for
$r \equiv 1 \pmod 4$ the minimal burden is $2$, identical in structure to
the $r = 5$ case, while for $r \equiv 3 \pmod 4$ every admissible $d_p$
gives burden $\ge 3$.
\item[(d)] The minimal burden as a function of $r$ is
\[
\mathrm{burden}(r) \;=\; \min_{\substack{d \mid r-1\\ d \ge 3}}
\begin{cases} d/2, & d \text{ even}\\ d, & d \text{ odd}\end{cases}
\]
which reproduces the measured A11 table for all eleven moduli tested there
(Section~\ref{sec:numerics}).
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The support statement is \eqref{eq:support} plus
Proposition~\ref{prop:sharp}; the dimension computation is
$\sum_{e \mid m}\varphi(e) = m$ with $m = d_p'$, together with
$\dim T_e = \varphi(e)$ and $\#T_e(\F_p) = \Phi_e(p)$
(Section~\ref{sec:setup}). (a): $d_p \ge 3$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:frob}(iii); $d_p' = 1$ requires $d_p \le 2$. (b):
$d_p' = 2$ with $d_p$ even means $d_p = 4$ and levels $\{e : e \mid 2\} =
\{1,2\}$; $d_p' = 2$ with $d_p$ odd is impossible ($d_p = 2$ is even). With
$p$ odd, $B$-smoothness of $p \mp 1 = 2m, 2(m+1)$ is twin-smoothness of the
pair $(m, m+1)$ up to the factor $2$. (c): $d_p = 4$ requires
$4 \mid \#(\Z/r)^\times = r-1$; conversely if $4 \mid r-1$ the cyclic group
$(\Z/r)^\times$ has elements of order $4$, and primes $p$ in those residue
classes exist (Dirichlet), with $\ord_r(n) = 4 \mid d_p$ satisfiable.
For $r \equiv 3 \pmod 4$: even $d \mid r-1$ satisfy
$d \equiv 2 \pmod 4$, so even $d \ge 3$ means $d \ge 6$, burden
$d/2 \ge 3$; odd $d \ge 3$ gives burden $d \ge 3$. (d) is the minimization
of $d_p'$ over admissible $d_p$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[structural closure of option (c)]\label{cor:optc}
For every AKS modulus $r$, the L--P mechanism requires each pool prime to
satisfy a smoothness condition on the point-orders of a torus of dimension
at least $2$; no modulus reduces the requirement to a single
one-dimensional torus (a single condition of the shape ``$p \pm 1$
smooth''). The minimum, dimension exactly $2$, is achieved precisely by the
split/nonsplit pair $\{T_1, T_2\}$ --- the twin-smooth double condition ---
and only for $r \equiv 1 \pmod 4$. The double condition measured
empirically in A11 is therefore intrinsic: it is the unique minimizer of a
dimension count, not an artifact of $r = 5$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[cost interpretation]\label{rem:cost}
Under the standard Dickman heuristic, $B$-smoothness of $\Phi_e(p)$, a
quantity of size $p^{\varphi(e)}$, has cost governed by
$u = \varphi(e)\log p / \log B$; total burden across the level set is
governed by $\sum_{e \mid d'}\varphi(e) = d'$. Burden $2$ is the
twin-smooth regime this project has harvested at scale; burden $\ge 3$
requires smooth values of $\Phi_e(p) \sim p^{\varphi(e)}$ with
$\varphi(e) \ge 2$, the same quantities whose \emph{non}-smoothness
underpins torus-based cryptography \cite{RS}. This is the structural
content of A11's ``every other $r$ is strictly worse''.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[what remains conditional]\label{rem:conditional}
Lemmas~\ref{lem:frob} and \ref{lem:collapse}, the support bound
\eqref{eq:support}, and parts (a)--(d) of Theorem~\ref{thm:dimcount} as
statements about the support of $\rho_r(p)$ are unconditional. The
conditional ingredient is generic sharpness
(Proposition~\ref{prop:sharp}): a harvest could in principle target the
thin set of primes where $\rho_r(p)$ collapses below \eqref{eq:support}.
Such primes have heuristic density $O(1/\ell)$ per large prime $\ell$
avoided --- i.e.\ the ``collapsed'' pool is far thinner than the twin-smooth
pool itself --- so this escape is uneconomical, but it is recorded here as
the localized gap per gate AG-G1 (\texttt{doc/arith-geom-plan.md}).
\end{remark}

\section{A quantified heuristic for Popovych's conjecture}\label{sec:popovych}

Popovych's conjecture \cite{Popovych09} strengthens the hypothesis of
Agrawal's to the simultaneous pair
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pair}
(X-1)^n \equiv X^n - 1
\quad\text{and}\quad
(X+2)^n \equiv X^n + 2 \pmod{n,\, X^r - 1},
\end{equation}
with the same conclusion. Beyond the Primaboinca search range ($n < 10^{17}$)
there is no published evidence in either direction. This section quantifies
what the second section costs the L--P mechanism --- the only known
counterexample engine --- and finds that it destroys exactly the structure
that made Agrawal's conjecture attackable: the inversion collapse of
Lemma~\ref{lem:collapse}, and with it the twin-smooth (burden-$2$) regime.

Throughout, $r \ne 3$ is prime and $p \nmid 3(2^r+1)$; the finitely many
excluded $p$ are degenerate ($p = 3$ makes $-2 \equiv 1$, so the two sections
of \eqref{eq:pair} \emph{coincide} mod $3$; $p \mid (2^r+1)/3$ makes $X+2$ a
zero divisor in some component).

\begin{lemma}[the level-one component is base-$3$ Fermat]\label{lem:base3}
The $X = 1$ component of the second congruence in \eqref{eq:pair} is
$3^n \equiv 3 \pmod n$. In particular it is automatic for any Carmichael-type
$n$ ($p - 1 \mid n - 1$ for all $p \mid n$), hence free for the L--P
construction; but unlike the first section, whose $X=1$ component is
$0 = 0$, the second section is already nontrivial at level one.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Evaluate \eqref{eq:pair} at $X = 1$: $(1+2)^n \equiv 1 + 2 \pmod{n}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[global independence]\label{lem:indep}
In $\Z[\zeta_r]$ the elements $\zeta - 1$ and $\zeta + 2$ have principal
ideals with disjoint prime support: $N(\zeta - 1) = \Phi_r(1) = r$ while
$N(\zeta + 2) = \Phi_r(-2) = (2^r+1)/3$, and
$\gcd\bigl(r,\, 2^r + 1\bigr) = \gcd(r, 3) = 1$. Since neither is a unit,
$\zeta - 1$ and $\zeta + 2$ generate a rank-$2$ subgroup of
$\Q(\zeta_r)^\times$ modulo roots of unity: no multiplicative identity ties
the order of one reduction to the order of the other.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$X^r - 1 = (X-1)\Phi_r(X)$ gives $\Phi_r(-2) = ((-2)^r - 1)/(-3) =
(2^r+1)/3$. By Fermat $2^r \equiv 2 \pmod r$, so $2^r + 1 \equiv 3
\pmod r$ and $\gcd(r, 2^r+1) = \gcd(r,3) = 1$ as $r \ne 3$. Disjoint
support plus non-unit norms gives independence modulo torsion.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[trivial stabilizer: no collapse at any depth]\label{lem:nocollapse}
For every $\gamma \in (\Z/r)^\times$ with $\gamma \ne 1$, the ratio
$\sigma_\gamma(\zeta+2)/(\zeta+2) = (\zeta^\gamma + 2)/(\zeta + 2)$ is not a
root of unity --- it is not even a unit of $\Z[\zeta_r]$. Consequently the
mechanism of Lemma~\ref{lem:collapse} has no analogue for $X + 2$: no
relation $(X+2)^{p^e - 1} \in \mu_{2r}$ holds for any $e$ with
$p^e \not\equiv 1 \pmod r$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $\mathfrak q$ be a prime of $\Z[\zeta_r]$ dividing $(\zeta + 2)$, so
$\zeta \equiv -2 \pmod{\mathfrak q}$ and the image of $-2$ in the residue
field has multiplicative order $r$ (it is the image of $\zeta$). If
$\mathfrak q$ also divided $(\zeta^\gamma + 2)$ then $\zeta^\gamma \equiv
-2 \equiv \zeta$, so $\zeta^{\gamma - 1} \equiv 1 \pmod{\mathfrak q}$,
forcing $r \mid \gamma - 1$, i.e.\ $\gamma = 1$. So for $\gamma \ne 1$ the
ratio has nonzero valuation at $\mathfrak q$ and is not a unit.

Contrast with the first section: $\sigma_{-1}(\zeta - 1)/(\zeta - 1) =
-\zeta^{-1} \in \mu_{2r}$, available because the ideal $(\zeta - 1)$ (the
totally ramified prime above $r$) is fixed by the whole Galois group ---
geometrically, the zero locus $\{1\}$ of $X - 1$ is Galois-stable, while
the zero $-2$ of $X + 2$ has trivial stabilizer.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[full-level support of the second section]\label{cor:fullsupport}
Define $\rho'_r(p)$ as the multiplicative order of $X + 2$ in
$(\F_p[X]/\Phi_r(X))^\times$. Then $\rho'_r(p) \mid p^d - 1 =
\prod_{e \mid d}\Phi_e(p)$, and --- in the generic-sharpness sense of
Proposition~\ref{prop:sharp}, now justified globally by
Lemma~\ref{lem:nocollapse} --- its support includes \emph{all} levels
$e \mid d$, in particular the top level $\Phi_d(p)$ of dimension
$\varphi(d)$, for even $d$ the level the first section never sees.
\end{corollary}

\begin{theorem}[Popovych burden]\label{thm:popburden}
Run the L--P mechanism for the pair \eqref{eq:pair}: for each $p \mid n$,
\[
n \equiv p^{\,j_p} \pmod{\lcm\bigl(r,\ \rho_r(p),\ \rho'_r(p)\bigr)},
\qquad \ord_r(n) > 2 .
\]
Under generic sharpness for both sections, the combined support is all
levels $e \mid d_p$, so the smoothness burden at $p$ is
$\sum_{e \mid d_p} \varphi(e) = d_p$ for \emph{every} parity of $d_p$.
Hence
\[
\mathrm{burden}_{\mathrm{Pop}}(r) \;=\; \min\{\, d : d \mid r-1,\ d \ge 3
\,\} \;\ge\; 3 \qquad \text{for every prime } r,
\]
and:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] the global optimum is $3$ (attained iff $3 \mid r-1$), versus
$2$ for the single section (Theorem~\ref{thm:dimcount});
\item[(b)] at $r = 5$ --- Popovych's own proposed modulus --- and generally
whenever $4 \mid r - 1$ and $3 \nmid r-1$, the burden is $4$: the pool
primes must have $p-1$, $p+1$ \emph{and} $p^2+1$ simultaneously smooth;
\item[(c)] $\mathrm{burden}_{\mathrm{Pop}}(r) \ge
\mathrm{burden}_{\mathrm{Agr}}(r)$ for every $r$, strictly whenever
$\mathrm{burden}_{\mathrm{Agr}}(r) = 2$ (i.e.\ for every $r \equiv 1
\pmod 4$);
\item[(d)] the twin-smooth regime (burden $2$) --- the only regime this
project found supply-feasible (gates G1/G2) --- does not exist for the pair
at any modulus.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For odd $d$ both sections are supported on all $e \mid d$ already
(Section~\ref{sec:dimcount}), total $d$; the pair ties with the single
section there. For even $d$ the first section collapses to $e \mid d/2$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:collapse}) but the second does not
(Corollary~\ref{cor:fullsupport}), so the $\lcm$ spans all $e \mid d$,
total $\sum_{e \mid d}\varphi(e) = d$ --- the collapse is destroyed.
Admissibility $d \ge 3$ is Lemma~\ref{lem:frob}(iii), unchanged.
Minimizing $d$ over $d \mid r-1$, $d \ge 3$ gives the formula. (a): the
least divisor $\ge 3$ of $r-1$ is $3$ iff $3 \mid r-1$. (b): if
$3 \nmid r-1$ and $4 \mid r-1$ the least is $4$, with levels
$\{1,2,4\}$ and $\Phi_4(p) = p^2+1$. (c):
$\mathrm{burden}_{\mathrm{Agr}}(r)$ minimizes $d'$ where $d' \le d$
always, so it is $\le \mathrm{burden}_{\mathrm{Pop}}(r)$; when it equals
$2$ the Popovych value is $\ge 3$ by the displayed bound. (d) is (a) plus
Theorem~\ref{thm:dimcount}(b).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[cost at the measured scale]\label{prop:popcost}
At the frozen harvest spec of this project (\texttt{doc/harvest-spec.md}:
pool primes $p \approx 51$ bits, measured supply/demand ratio $13.8$, pool
$2.5\times 10^5$), the extra quadratic level of
Theorem~\ref{thm:popburden}(b) is a pure Dickman tax on a
$\approx 102$-bit quantity: $\rho(u)$ with $u = 102/\log_2 B$, i.e.
\[
u \approx 9.9,\ \rho(u) \approx 3.9\times10^{-11} \quad (B = 1259),
\qquad
u \approx 7.5,\ \rho(u) \approx 1.8\times10^{-7} \quad (B' = 12590),
\]
multiplying the measured ratio down to $5\times10^{-10}$--$2.5\times10^{-6}$
and the pool to $10^{-5}$--$0.05$ elements: \emph{the pair-mechanism harvest
is empty at any feasible scale}. Unlike $p \mp 1$, where partition-first
harvesting lets one side be enumerated rather than tested
(\texttt{data/FINDINGS.md}, A1), $\Phi_4(p)$ is pinned once $p$ is, so no
enumeration trick removes this tax. On the solver side the group acquires
the level-$4$ components, so $\log_2|G|$ grows and the identity density
falls below the measured $\rho \approx 0.004$ of H7 pools: the N1/A10
obstruction for the pair strictly contains the one for the single section.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[interpretation and scope]\label{rem:popscope}
Theorem~\ref{thm:popburden} and Proposition~\ref{prop:popcost} are, to our
knowledge, the first quantified evidence in either direction on Popovych's
conjecture beyond exhaustive search: every known counterexample mechanism
pays at least one more torus dimension against the pair than against the
single section globally ($3$ vs.\ $2$), and two more at the canonical
$r = 5$ ($4$ vs.\ $2$); the burden-$2$ regime that made the Agrawal attack
supply-feasible does not exist for the pair. The caveats are those of
Remarks~\ref{rem:scope} and \ref{rem:conditional}, applied to both
sections: this quantifies the failure of the only known engine, not the
truth of the conjecture. Note also the char-$3$ degeneracy: modulo $p = 3$
the two sections coincide, so the pair constrains only $p > 3$ divisors.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[the constant $2$ is immaterial]\label{rem:constant}
For any integer $c \notin \{0, \pm 1\}$ the argument of
Lemma~\ref{lem:nocollapse} runs verbatim for the section $X + c$: at a
prime $\mathfrak q \mid (\zeta + c)$ the image of $-c$ has multiplicative
order $r$, so no $\gamma \ne 1$ preserves the divisor, the stabilizer is
trivial, and the full burden jump of Theorem~\ref{thm:popburden} holds
with $c$ entering only through the norm $\Phi_r(-c) = (c^r + 1)/(c + 1)$
(whose primes, with $p \mid c(c+1)$, are the finitely many excluded $p$),
the excluded modulus $r \mid c + 1$ (for $c = 2$: $r = 3$), and the
level-one component, the base-$(c{+}1)$ Fermat condition. What must be
avoided is exactly degeneracy of the zero $-c$: it must be a point of
$\Gm$ avoiding the rational torsion $\mu_2(\Q) = \{\pm 1\}$, i.e.\
$c \notin \{0, \pm 1\}$. The exclusion $c = 0$ is vacuous rather than
subtle ($X$ is a monomial, so $(X)^n \equiv X^n$ is an identity in every
ring; geometrically the zero sits at the origin, off the torus, and the
section is a unit of finite order $r$). Among nonzero $c$:
$c = -1$ is the first section itself, and $c = +1$ --- the one
innocent-looking bad choice --- has its zero at $-1$, fixed by inversion,
so the collapse \emph{survives}: $(\zeta + 1)^{p^{d/2}} =
\zeta^{-1}(\zeta + 1)$, giving $\ord(X + 1) \mid r(p^{d/2} - 1)$ for even
$d$; worse, $\zeta + 1 = (\zeta^2 - 1)/(\zeta - 1)$ is multiplicatively
dependent on the Galois orbit of $\zeta - 1$, so the pair
$\{X - 1, X + 1\}$ adds no rank and no burden at all --- a Popovych-style
conjecture with $c = 1$ would be no harder for the mechanism than
Agrawal's. In the language of Theorem~\ref{thm:stabindex}:
$U(X + c) = \{\pm 1\}$ for $c = \pm 1$ and $\{1\}$ otherwise; $c = 2$ is
simply the smallest valid choice ($c = -2$ works equally, with Mersenne
norm $2^r - 1$). Verified numerically
(\texttt{code/ag5b\_constant\_check.py}): at even $d$ the section $X + 1$
collapses in $22/22$ samples with the top level never needed, while
$c \in \{2, 3, -2, 5\}$ collapse only at the single degenerate point
$(r, p) = (13, 5)$, where $\Phi_4(5) = 26$ has no prime outside $2r$ and
the top level is vacuous for every section.
\end{remark}


\begin{proposition}[the $X+1$ variant is mechanism-equivalent to
Agrawal's conjecture]\label{prop:xplus1}
Suppose $n \equiv p^{j} \pmod{\lcm(r, \rho_r(p))}$ and $p - 1 \mid n - 1$
(the mechanism congruence together with the Carmichael condition, both
imposed by every L--P-style construction). Then $T(1, n, r)$ holds modulo
$p$ outright. Consequently every counterexample to Agrawal's conjecture
produced by the mechanism also defeats the variant conjecture whose
hypothesis is the pair $\{T(-1,n,r),\, T(1,n,r)\}$: that variant, though
formally stronger-looking, is exactly as resistant as Agrawal's --- the
second hypothesis is free armor --- whereas $c = 2$ (or any
$c \notin \{0, \pm 1\}$) genuinely blocks the engine.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
At the $X = 1$ component, $(1+1)^n = 2^n \equiv 2 \pmod p$ since
$\ord_p(2) \mid p - 1 \mid n - 1$. In the $\Phi_r$ components,
$\zeta + 1 = (\zeta^2 - 1)/(\zeta - 1)$, a unit (its norm is
$\Phi_r(-1) = 1$), and both $\zeta - 1$ and $\zeta^2 - 1$ are values of
$X - 1$ at primitive $r$-th roots of unity, so their orders divide
$\rho_r(p) = \lcm_a \ord(\zeta^a - 1)$. Hence
$(\zeta^a - 1)^n = (\zeta^a - 1)^{p^j} = \zeta^{a p^j} - 1$ for
$a = 1, 2$, and
\[
(\zeta + 1)^n \;=\; \frac{(\zeta^2 - 1)^n}{(\zeta - 1)^n}
\;=\; \frac{\zeta^{2p^j} - 1}{\zeta^{p^j} - 1}
\;=\; \zeta^{p^j} + 1 \;=\; \zeta^n + 1,
\]
using $n \equiv p^j \pmod r$ in the last step. Measured
(\texttt{code/ag5b\_constant\_check.py}, check W): $48/48$ synthetic
mechanism instances pass $T(1, n, r)$, while $0/48$ pass $T(2, n, r)$.
\end{proof}

\section{The impersonation hierarchy}\label{sec:hierarchy}

Theorems~\ref{thm:dimcount} and \ref{thm:popburden} are statements about
which congruence cells a counterexample mechanism forces its pool primes
into. This section defines those cells once and for all, maps the classical
generalized-Carmichael literature onto them, and records what is known about
each --- the related-work backbone and vocabulary for the negative-result
paper (\texttt{doc/negative-result-plan.md}).

\begin{definition}[impersonation cells]\label{def:cells}
Let $p$ be prime and $n$ coprime to $p$. For a finite set $S$ of levels and
powers $\mathbf i = (i_e)_{e \in S}$, say \emph{$n$ impersonates Frobenius
at $p$ on $S$ with powers $\mathbf i$} if
\[
\Phi_e(p) \mid n - p^{\,i_e} \qquad \text{for every } e \in S .
\]
By the dictionary of Section~\ref{sec:setup} this says exactly that
$x^n = x^{p^{i_e}}$ for all $x$ in the order-$\Phi_e(p)$ subgroup of
$\F_{p^e}^\times$ --- the points of the torus $T_e$. Uniform-power
impersonation at \emph{depth} $m$ is the single congruence
$p^m - 1 \mid n - p^i$, i.e.\ $x^n = x^{p^i}$ for all
$x \in \F_{p^m}^\times$. A squarefree composite $n$ \emph{lies in a cell}
when every $p \mid n$ impersonates accordingly. Note two normalizations:
at level $1$ all powers coincide ($p \equiv 1 \bmod p-1$), so only the cell
membership matters there; and on $T_2$ one has $p^i \equiv (-1)^i
\pmod{p+1}$, so at level $2$ only the \emph{parity} of $i_2$ matters ---
the parity is a sign: even $\leftrightarrow$ target $+1$, odd
$\leftrightarrow$ target $-1$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[the classical objects in cell coordinates]\label{prop:celldict}
For squarefree composite $n$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] $n$ is a Carmichael number (Korselt: $p - 1 \mid n - 1$ for all
$p \mid n$) iff $n$ lies in the cell $(\{1\}, 0)$.
\item[(ii)] $n$ is a Lucas--Carmichael number ($p + 1 \mid n + 1$) iff $n$
lies in $(\{2\}, 1)$: indeed $p + 1 \mid n - p \iff p + 1 \mid n + 1$.
\item[(iii)] $n$ is a Williams number \cite{Williams77,Echi07,McIntoshMitra}
(both conditions) iff $n$ lies in $(\{1,2\}, (0,1))$, equivalently iff $n$
impersonates at depth $2$ with the uniform power $1$:
$n \equiv p \pmod{(p^2-1)/2}$ for every $p \mid n$.
\item[(iv)] $n$ is a Carmichael number of order $m$ in the sense of Howe
\cite{Howe00} ($x \mapsto x^n$ an endomorphism of every $\Z/n$-algebra
generated by $m$ elements) iff for every $p \mid n$ and every depth
$r \le m$ some power works: $\exists i,\ p^r - 1 \mid n - p^i$ (Howe's
Theorem~1). For $m = 2$ this is: Carmichael, plus level-$2$ membership with
\emph{some} parity. Howe's \emph{rigid} numbers are the all-powers-$0$
(all-even-parity) case $\lcm_{r \le m}(p^r - 1) \mid n - 1$.
\item[(v)] The Lenstra--Pomerance pool for Agrawal's conjecture is the
Williams cell refined by the local conditions ($p \equiv 3 \bmod 80$,
validity $n \not\equiv \pm1 \bmod r$); Theorem~\ref{thm:dimcount} says the
AKS mechanism at any modulus $r$ forces the level set $\{e : e \mid d'\}$,
with the Williams cell $\{1,2\}$ as the unique dimension-$2$ minimum;
Theorem~\ref{thm:popburden} says the Popovych pair forces the full level
set $\{e : e \mid d\}$. Grantham's Frobenius pseudoprimes \cite{Grantham}
are the same impersonation idea tested against a single polynomial section
rather than all torus points.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i)--(iii) unwind Definition~\ref{def:cells} ((iii): $n \equiv 1 \bmod p-1$
and $n \equiv -1 \equiv p \bmod p+1$ combine by CRT to
$n \equiv p \bmod \lcm(p-1,p+1) = (p^2-1)/2$); the point-identity form of
the dictionary is verified numerically in Section~\ref{sec:hiernumerics}.
(iv) is Howe's Theorem~1 \cite{Howe00} together with the level-$1$
normalization above. (v) collects Sections~\ref{sec:dimcount} and
\ref{sec:popovych}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[status of the cells --- survey]\label{thm:status}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] $(\{1\}, 0)$, Carmichael: infinitely many \cite{AGP}; $105$
below $10^7$.
\item[(b)] $(\{2\}, 1)$, Lucas--Carmichael: infinitely many --- Wright
\cite{Wright18}, as a by-product of the infinitude of elliptic Carmichael
numbers; $135$ below $10^7$.
\item[(c)] $(\{1,2\}, (0,1))$, Williams: open whether the cell is even
nonempty. Williams \cite{Williams77} posed the question and showed such
$n$ need at least four prime factors; McIntosh--Mitra \cite{McIntoshMitra}
verified none exist below $2^{64} \approx 1.8\times10^{19}$ and proved
finiteness results (only finitely many with a given set of all but three
prime factors). The Agrawal cell (v) is a refinement, a fortiori empty
there --- consistent with OEIS A329223's emptiness against Pinch's tables.
\item[(d)] Depth-$2$, any parity (Howe order $2$): \emph{nonempty}. Howe's
explicit example $n = 443372888629441 = 17\cdot31\cdot41\cdot43\cdot89
\cdot97\cdot167\cdot331$ is rigid of order $2$ ($p^2 - 1 \mid n - 1$ for
all eight primes; re-verified here, Section~\ref{sec:hiernumerics}). Every
order-$m$ Carmichael number has at least $m + 2$ prime factors
\cite{EterevskyVsemirnov}; no order-$3$ example is known; Howe's heuristic
predicts infinitely many of every order.
\item[(e)] Elliptic rungs: Silverman's elliptic Korselt criteria
\cite{Silverman12}, conditional infinitude of elliptic Carmichael numbers
by Ekstrom--Pomerance--Thakur \cite{EPT}, unconditional by Wright
\cite{Wright18} --- the structures item AG4 will build on.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}[sign splitting, not depth, is the wall]\label{rem:signs}
Contrast (c) and (d): rigid depth-$2$ impersonators exist at
$4.4\times10^{14}$, while the Williams cell --- the \emph{same} level set
--- is empty to $2^{64}$. Depth is not the obstruction; the sign structure
is. The rigid cell's condition is one-sided: $n \equiv 1 \pmod{
\lcm_{p \mid n}(p^2-1)}$ is an AGP-type problem --- harvest primes with
$p^2 - 1 \mid \Lambda$ for a single highly composite $\Lambda$ (the $p-1$
and $p+1$ parts may share prime support freely) and solve one
subset-product with target $1$; that machine produced (d) at modest size.
The Williams parities $(0, 1)$ instead force target $+1$ on the level-$1$
side and $-1$ on the level-$2$ side; on any shared prime power the two
targets conflict, so the modulus must split into coprime sides --- exactly
the side-coprimality (working paper Lemma~2.3) whose harvest collapse this
project measured as N1 and whose two-sided solver obstruction is A10. In
cell language: the project's negative results concern precisely the
\emph{sign-split} cells, and Howe's example shows the unsplit cells are
reachable --- the empirical record (c) vs.\ (d) confirms the split, not the
depth, as the constructible/blocked boundary.

The AKS validity twist pins the construction to a sign-split cell: a
rigid-cell $n$ can satisfy the component equation of
Lemma~\ref{lem:frob}(ii) only with exponent $j = 0$, forcing $n \equiv 1
\pmod r$ --- inside the exception clause, not a counterexample (cf.\
V\'a\v{n}a's observation \cite{Vana} that Carmichael-type numbers pass
$T(-1,n,r)$ only in such classes). Concretely: Howe's rigid $n$ has
$n \equiv 1 \pmod 5$, yet it does not even pass $T(-1,n,5)$
(Section~\ref{sec:hiernumerics}): rigidity controls the $p^2-1$ part of
$\rho_5(p)$ but not the extra $\mu_{2r}$-torsion --- the factor $2r$ in
Lemma~\ref{lem:collapse}'s bound --- which is exactly the $2$-adic
bookkeeping the L--P conditions modulo $80$ exist to fix.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[imports for the negative-result paper]\label{rem:imports}
Three direct imports: (1) \emph{vocabulary} --- the paper's target objects
are Williams numbers in prescribed congruence cells, an established name
with a 1977 pedigree; (2) \emph{search floor} --- McIntosh--Mitra's
$2^{64}$ bound is the modern version of the Pinch-table emptiness the
README cites; (3) \emph{calibration} --- Howe's construction of (d) is the
nearest success of the AGP machine to our target cell, and the
one-sided/sign-split contrast of Remark~\ref{rem:signs} is the structural
explanation of why his succeeded where H8/N1 collapsed; any future attempt
on the Williams cell must engage that contrast, not the depth.
\end{remark}

\section{The elliptic axis: formulation and the stabilizer-index floor}
\label{sec:elliptic}

Everything measured in this project (A1--A11, N1) lives on $\Gm$ and its
tori. Item AG4 asks whether replacing $\mu_r \subset \Gm$ by elliptic
torsion opens a route the torus negative results do not cover. The answer,
reached here through stages (a) and (b) of the plan, is no: the burden
floor of $2$ is a structural invariant, and gate AG-G2 is not passed.

\subsection{Formulation (AG4a): the test needs CM}

The AKS congruence rests on the \emph{canonical} substitution
$\sigma_n : X \mapsto X^n$ --- the Adams operation of the $\Lambda$-ring
$\Z[\mu_r]$ (Section~\ref{sec:dictionary}). If an elliptic curve carried
such a canonical commuting family, each $\psi_p$ would be an endomorphism
of degree $p$ lifting Frobenius, and an endomorphism of non-square degree
already forces $\mathrm{End}(E) \supsetneq \Z$, i.e.\ complex
multiplication --- so only CM curves are eligible; conversely, for CM
curves the canonical family exists (Borger--Gurney \cite{BorgerGurney}).
For a non-CM curve
there is no analogue of $\sigma_n$ at all, and testing against all
candidate traces $a \in [-2\sqrt n, 2\sqrt n]$ is exponential. So the
well-posed analogue is CM:

\begin{definition}[elliptic AKS test, CM form]\label{def:elltest}
Let $E/\Q$ have CM by $O_K$, $K$ imaginary quadratic with unit group
$O_K^\times$ of order $w$ ($w = 4$: $j = 1728$, $E : y^2 = x^3 - x$,
$[i](x,y) = (-x, iy)$; $w = 6$: $j = 0$). Fix an odd prime $r$ of good
reduction split in $K$, a kernel polynomial $g$ of an $r$-eigenline
(certificate-style, or explicit for special sequences as in
\cite{GK12}), and a section $f$ (we take $f = x$). The test: for
composite-candidate $n$ coprime to $2r\,\Delta_E$,
\[
\exists\, \tau \in (\Z/r)^\times : \qquad
f^{\,n} \;\equiv\; f \circ [\tau] \pmod{n,\ g(x)},
\]
$[\tau]$ acting through division polynomials ($r - 1$ candidates, so
poly$(r)$ trials). For $n$ prime, ordinary and split, the $n$-power
Frobenius acts on the eigenline by a scalar $\lambda_n$ and the test
passes with $\tau = \lambda_n$.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[the exception clause]\label{rem:ellclause}
Let $U \le (\Z/r)^\times$ be the image of $O_K^\times$ (for $j = 1728$:
$U = \mu_4 = \{\pm1, \pm i\}$, present iff $4 \mid r-1$). Cells with
$\lambda_p \in U$ at every $p \mid n$ are the elliptic analogue of the
rigid cells of Section~\ref{sec:hierarchy}: their assembly is a one-sided
AGP-type problem (heuristically constructible, as Howe's example
demonstrates for the rigid torus cell), so a credible elliptic Agrawal
conjecture must read: \emph{the congruence forces $n$ prime or
$\tau \in U$} --- the clause is the stabilizer, exactly as
$n^2 \equiv 1 \pmod r$ is $\sigma_n \in \{\pm 1\}$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:clause}). Validity of a counterexample therefore means
$\lambda$-data escaping $U$.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The stabilizer-index theorem (AG4b)}

The collapse identities are exact, as in Lemma~\ref{lem:collapse}: for an
eigenline point $T$ with Frobenius scalar $\lambda$,
$x(T)^{p^e} = x([\lambda^e]T)$, and on $E : y^2 = x^3 - x$ the
automorphism gives $x([i]S) = -x(S)$ for \emph{all} $S$. Hence
$\lambda^e \equiv \pm 1$ gives $x(T)^{p^e} = x(T)$ and
$\lambda^e \equiv \pm i$ gives $x(T)^{p^e} = -x(T)$; with
\[
k' \;:=\; \min\{\, e \ge 1 : \lambda^e \in U \,\}
\;=\; [\langle\lambda\rangle : \langle\lambda\rangle \cap U],
\]
the order of $x(T)$ divides $2(p^{k'} - 1)$: support on the levels
$e \mid k'$, burden $k'$. The same computation in each earlier setting
yields one statement:

\begin{theorem}[stabilizer-index floor]\label{thm:stabindex}
Let a test section $f$ on a torsion scheme carry a scalar Frobenius
action $\lambda$ in a cyclic group $\Gamma$, and let $U \le \Gamma$ be
the subgroup whose action fixes $f$ up to root-of-unity cofactors (the
stabilizer of $\operatorname{div} f$ up to torsion units). Then the
$p$-dependent support of the control order is the levels
$e \mid k'$ with $k' = [\langle\lambda\rangle : \langle\lambda\rangle
\cap U]$ --- an unconditional upper bound via the exact cofactor
identities, attained generically in the sense of
Proposition~\ref{prop:sharp}. A valid counterexample requires
$\lambda \notin U$ (Remark~\ref{rem:ellclause} and its torus analogues),
hence $\langle\lambda\rangle \cap U \lneq \langle\lambda\rangle$ and
\[
\text{burden} \;=\; k' \;\ge\; 2 .
\]
Instances: $(\mu_r,\, X-1)$: $U = \{\pm1\}$, burden $d/\gcd(d,2)$
adjusted to $d'$ --- Theorem~\ref{thm:dimcount}; $(\mu_r,\, X+2)$:
$U = \{1\}$, burden $d$ --- Theorem~\ref{thm:popburden};
$(E_{j=1728},\, x)$: $U = \mu_4$, burden $m/\gcd(m, 4)$ for
$\ord(\lambda) = m$; $(E_{j=0},\, x)$: $U = \mu_6$-image, burden
$m/\gcd(m, 6)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Collapse: with $e = k'$ minimal, $\lambda^{k'} \in U$ gives
$f(T)^{p^{k'}} = \zeta f(T)$ with $\zeta$ a root of unity of small order
(the cofactor of the unit action on $f$: $-\zeta^{-1}$ for $X - 1$ under
inversion, $-1$ for $x$ under $[i]$, $\zeta_3^{\pm1}$ for $x$ under
$[\zeta_3]$), so $\ord f(T)$ divides $(\text{small}) \cdot (p^{k'}-1)$.
No smaller $e$ works: for $e < k'$, $\lambda^e \notin U$ and the cofactor
$f \circ [\lambda^e] / f$ is a non-torsion unit (cyclotomic for $\mu_r$,
Lemma~\ref{lem:nocollapse}; elliptic for $E$, where the division values
are supported above $r$ and distinct points give distinct prime support
--- the same disjoint-support argument), so no identity collapses the
order; generic sharpness then puts the full levels $e \mid k'$ in the
order. The index formula is cyclicity of $\langle\lambda\rangle$.
Validity excludes $\lambda \in U$, so the index is at least $2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[supersingular cells]
For $p$ inert in $K$ (e.g.\ $p \equiv 3 \bmod 4$ on $y^2 = x^3 - x$),
$a_p = 0$ and $\varphi^2 = [-p]$, so the collapse of $x$-values is
governed by $\langle p \rangle \subseteq (\Z/r)^\times$ exactly as in the
torus case --- supersingular cells offer no separate escape.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Gate AG-G2: not passed}

\begin{corollary}[the elliptic axis is closed]\label{cor:ellclosed}
On the $j = 1728$ curve the per-modulus burden is
$\mathrm{burden}_{\mathrm{ell}}(r) = \min\{\, m/\gcd(m,4) \ge 2 : m \mid
r - 1 \,\}$, degenerate (no valid cell) when $r - 1 \mid 4\cdot 2^\infty$
fails to provide one, e.g.\ $r = 5$; on $j = 0$ replace $4$ by $6$. In
every tested case (Section~\ref{sec:ellnumerics})
$\mathrm{burden}_{\mathrm{ell}}(r) \ge \mathrm{burden}_{\mathrm{Agr}}(r)$,
and the floor $2$ --- attained iff $8 \mid r-1$ ($j = 1728$) or
$12 \mid r-1$ ($j = 0$) --- lands on the levels $\{1, 2\}$: \emph{$p - 1$
and $p + 1$ smooth, the same twin-smooth demand as the torus route}, now
with a ray-class slice ($\lambda_p$ of order $2w$ mod $r$, a positive-%
density Chebotarev condition) in place of the mod-$80$ cell, and with
unit-twisted mixed targets ($x(T)^{p^{k'}} = -x(T)$), i.e.\ the
sign-split structure of Remark~\ref{rem:signs} --- so the N1/A10
harvest-and-solver obstruction transfers verbatim. The apparent
burden-$1$ cells ($\lambda \in U$) are exactly the exception clause.
Gate AG-G2 of the plan is therefore \textbf{not passed}: the elliptic
axis offers no single-rare-event escape, and stage AG4(c) is cancelled;
this section extends the negative result to the CM-elliptic axis.
\end{corollary}

Caveats, beyond the standing ones (mechanism scope, Remark~\ref{rem:scope};
generic sharpness, Remark~\ref{rem:conditional}): the formulation itself is
CM-bound --- the non-CM "elliptic Agrawal" is not well-posed for lack of a
canonical Frobenius lift \cite{BorgerGurney} --- and the eigenline kernel
polynomial is certificate-style data for general $n$ (explicit only for
special sequences, as in \cite{GK12,CouveignesLercier}).

\section{The function-field mirror (item AG6, sketch)}\label{app:ff}

Over $A = \F_q[t]$ every object of this document has a classical mirror,
and the comparison isolates what our two heuristic caveats --- generic
sharpness (Proposition~\ref{prop:sharp}) and the L--P-style density counts
--- are standing in for over $\Z$.

The mirror of $\Gm$ with its Adams operations is the \emph{Carlitz module}
$C : A \to \mathrm{End}(\mathbb G_a)$, $C_t(x) = tx + x^q$: the maps
$C_a$ commute, and for a monic irreducible $P$ of degree $d$ one has the
Frobenius-lift congruence $C_P(x) \equiv x^{q^d} \pmod P$ --- here a
\emph{theorem} of the module rather than a test. The mirror of
$\Z[\mu_r]$ is the Carlitz cyclotomic ring $A[x]/(C_R(x))$ for an
auxiliary irreducible $R$, whose torsion values generate the cyclotomic
function fields (Carlitz--Hayes theory). The mechanism analysis of
Sections~\ref{sec:setup}--\ref{sec:dimcount} transposes: residue fields
of torsion values are constant-field extensions $\F_{q^{de}}$, their unit
orders factor through the same cyclotomic levels, and the stabilizer-index
principle of Theorem~\ref{thm:stabindex} applies with $U$ the image of
the constants $\F_q^\times$, which act on the identity section with
monomial cofactors ($C_c(x) = cx$).

Two ingredients change epistemic status. (i) The sharpness questions ---
which large primes divide the order of the reduction of a fixed torsion
value as $P$ varies --- become effective: Frobenius equidistribution in
the relevant cyclotomic-function-field extensions holds with square-root
error by Weil's RH for curves (function-field Chebotarev), which is
exactly the Artin-type input that keeps Proposition~\ref{prop:sharp}
heuristic over $\Z$. (ii) The existence side (primes $P$ in prescribed
cells with smooth order data) becomes quantitative for the same reason.
Yet the mirror carries no complexity content: irreducibility testing in
$\F_q[t]$ is unconditionally deterministic polynomial time (Frobenius
linear algebra: $x^{q^n} \equiv x$ and gcd conditions), so no
Agrawal-type conjecture is needed there. The conclusion of the comparison
is a placement, not a plan: \emph{the mechanism analysis of this document
is complete in itself, and the part of the Agrawal problem that resists
it over $\Z$ is exactly the unconditional-equidistribution gap that Weil
closes over $\F_q[t]$} --- a GRH-grade input, consistent with the fact
that the best unconditional test \cite{LPGauss} works by constructing its
Frobenius datum rather than certifying it from one section. We make no
claims here about Carlitz analogues of the Carmichael hierarchy of
Section~\ref{sec:hierarchy}; that literature is not needed for the
placement above.
