\begin{abstract}
Agrawal's conjecture asserts that for a prime $r \nmid n$, the congruence
$(X-1)^n \equiv X^n - 1 \pmod{n,\,X^r-1}$ forces $n$ prime or
$n^2 \equiv 1 \pmod r$; if true it yields a deterministic
$\tilde O(\log^3 n)$ primality test. The only known route to a
counterexample is the Lenstra--Pomerance construction: a squarefree $n$
whose prime factors $p$ satisfy $p - 1 \mid n - 1$ and $p + 1 \mid n + 1$
with auxiliary local conditions. We report on a sustained attempt to
\emph{construct} such an $n$ of unrestricted size, as a two-stage pipeline:
harvest a pool of primes $p = 2m+1$ with $(m, m+1)$ twin-smooth over
partitioned factor bases, then solve a two-sided $0/1$ subset-product in
$(\Z/\Lambda_-)^\times \times (\Z/\Lambda_+)^\times$. The supply stage is
solved: a partition-first harvester achieves measured supply/demand ratios
of $13.8$--$209$ against a yield model validated (conservatively) on the
complete $B = 547$ twin-smooth corpus. The solve stage is blocked, by a
\emph{measured, two-part obstruction}: pools dense enough for the only
scalable subset-product solver family (the Alford--Granville--Pomerance /
L\"oh--Niebuhr / AGHS methods, which need identity-density
$\rho \gtrsim 0.98$ by a measured generalized-birthday reach law
$r_{\max} \sim \log N / (1 - \rho)$) are unharvestable under the double
condition, while harvestable pools have $\rho \approx 0.004$. We then show
the obstruction is structural rather than parametric: a torus
dimension-count theorem proves the double (twin-smooth) condition is the
unique minimum-burden cell for \emph{every} AKS modulus $r$; Popovych's
two-section variant is strictly harder (burden $\ge 3$ everywhere, $4$ at
$r = 5$); and a stabilizer-index theorem extends the same floor to the
CM-elliptic analogue. The structural statements are unconditional modulo
one named equidistribution-type hypothesis --- Artin-type \emph{generic
sharpness}, the attainment of the proved support bounds --- empirically
supported (zero exceptions across $196$ sampled large primes) and a
theorem in the function-field mirror by Weil's RH for curves; the
restriction to Frobenius-matching mechanisms is a separate, stated scope.
This is not a disproof of the conjecture, nor a proof
that no counterexample can be constructed; it is a quantified closure of
the known constructive route, with the residual open directions stated
precisely.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

For coprime integers $n, r$ write $T(-1,n,r)$ for the congruence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:aks}
(X-1)^n \equiv X^n - 1 \pmod{n,\, X^r-1}.
\end{equation}

\begin{conjecture}[Agrawal; Bhattacharjee--Pandey \cite{BP01}]\label{conj:agrawal}
If $r$ is a prime not dividing $n$ and $T(-1,n,r)$ holds, then $n$ is prime
or $n^2 \equiv 1 \pmod r$.
\end{conjecture}

If true, a single evaluation of \eqref{eq:aks} at one $r = O(\log n)$ would
certify primality deterministically in $\tilde O(\log^3 n)$ time, against
$\tilde O(\log^6 n)$ for the best proven algorithm \cite{LP2019}. The
conjecture's status is peculiar: Lenstra and Pomerance \cite{LPremarks}
gave a heuristic argument for $\gg x^{1-\varepsilon}$ counterexamples below
$x$, later extended by Popovych \cite{Popovych09} and Hegde--Devaraj
\cite{HD21}, yet no counterexample is known, exhaustive search is clean to
$10^{17}$ (Primaboinca, 2010--2020), and folklore places the least
counterexample at hundreds of digits.

Searching is therefore hopeless, but \emph{constructing} is not obviously
so: the Lenstra--Pomerance (L--P) mechanism is deterministic --- when its
arithmetic side conditions hold, \eqref{eq:aks} follows with no further
luck --- and its heuristic density \emph{improves} with size. This paper
reports the outcome of carrying that program through, with every stage
instrumented and measured. Our contributions:

\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt
\item[(i)] \textbf{Supply is solved.} A \emph{partition-first} harvester
for the required pool primes achieves measured supply/demand ratios of
$13.8$--$209$ (gate threshold $2.5$), with a per-candidate yield model
validated against the complete $B = 547$ twin-smooth corpus
(predicted/actual $0.41$--$1.04$, geometric mean $0.68$: conservative).
Along the way we quantify why the naive approach fails: mining any fixed
twin-smooth corpus hits a measured \emph{$0.06$ invariant} ($\approx 0.06$
usable elements per demand bit, a factor $16$--$25$ below feasibility),
stable across corpora from $78$ to $73{,}163$ pool elements
(\S\ref{sec:supply}).
\item[(ii)] \textbf{The solver stage is blocked by a measured density
gap.} The group's $2$-part is $\mathrm{GF}(2)$-linear and solved at full
scale, but carries only ${\sim}16\%$ of the constraint bits. For the odd
part, generic solvers cap $2$--$3$ orders of magnitude below the required
dimension; the only scalable family (AGP/L\"oh--Niebuhr/AGHS
subset-product methods \cite{AGP,LN,AGHS}) requires pool identity-density
$\rho \gtrsim 0.98$ by a measured generalized-birthday reach law, while
pools harvestable under the double Carmichael + Lucas--Carmichael
condition have $\rho \approx 0.004$, and pools with AGHS-grade density are
measurably unharvestable ($0$--$2$ elements at every feasible scale). One
can have a harvestable pool or a solvable one, not both
(\S\ref{sec:solver}).
\item[(iii)] \textbf{The obstruction is structural, not parametric.} A
torus dimension-count theorem shows that for \emph{every} AKS modulus $r$
the mechanism forces smoothness control on cyclotomic tori of total
dimension $\ge 2$, with the minimum $2$ attained exactly by the
split/nonsplit pair --- the twin-smooth double condition --- so no choice
of $r$ escapes (i); Popovych's variant is strictly harder (burden $\ge 3$
everywhere, $4$ at his own $r = 5$); and a stabilizer-index theorem
extends the same floor to the CM-elliptic analogue of the test
(\S\ref{sec:structural}).
\item[(iv)] \textbf{Reusable machinery and vocabulary.} A validated
near-complete twin-smooth generator, the yield model, the density-reach
diagnostic for Carmichael-type constructions, and the identification of
the target objects with the \emph{Williams numbers} of the generalized-%
Carmichael literature \cite{Williams77,Echi07,McIntoshMitra}
(\S\ref{sec:conclusion}).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{What is not claimed.} This paper does not disprove
Conjecture~\ref{conj:agrawal}, which remains open and widely believed
false. It does not prove that no counterexample can ever be constructed.
Its negative results quantify the failure of the \emph{known mechanism}
(Frobenius-matching constructions in the L--P family) when driven by the
\emph{known solver families}; \S\ref{sec:scope} states the boundary of the
claim and the open directions precisely. The empirical statements are
measurements, reproducible from the committed code and data
(Appendix~\ref{app:repro}); the structural theorems of
\S\ref{sec:structural} are proved, with one generic-sharpness ingredient
whose status (heuristic, empirically supported, Artin-type) is flagged
wherever used.

\section{The construction and its reduction}\label{sec:reduction}

\begin{theorem}[Lenstra--Pomerance \cite{LPremarks}; the $k \equiv 3 \bmod
4$ case in V\'a\v{n}a \cite{Vana09}]\label{thm:lp}
Let $n = p_1 \cdots p_k$ with $p_1, \dots, p_k$ distinct primes such that
\emph{(a)} $k$ is odd; \emph{(b)} $p_i \equiv 3 \pmod{80}$;
\emph{(c)} $p_i - 1 \mid n - 1$ for all $i$ ($n$ is Carmichael);
\emph{(d)} $p_i + 1 \mid n + 1$ for all $i$ ($n$ is Lucas--Carmichael).
Then $T(-1,n,5)$ holds and $n^2 \not\equiv 1 \pmod 5$; such an $n$ with
$k \ge 3$ is a counterexample to Conjecture~\ref{conj:agrawal}.
\end{theorem}

The mechanism behind Theorem~\ref{thm:lp} is \emph{Frobenius matching}:
modulo each $p \mid n$, the ring $\F_p[X]/(X^5-1)$ splits as $\F_p \times
\F_{p^4}$, the Frobenius gives $(\zeta - 1)^{p^j} = \zeta^{p^j} - 1$
exactly, and conditions (b)--(d) force $n \equiv p \pmod{\lcm(5,
\rho_5(p))}$, where $\rho_5(p)$, the multiplicative order of $X - 1$ in
$(\F_p[X]/\Phi_5)^\times$, divides $10(p^2 - 1)$ \cite{Vana09}. Composite
$n$ built this way \emph{impersonate the Frobenius} of each prime divisor
on both one-dimensional algebraic tori over $\F_p$: the split torus
($\#\mathbb{G}_m(\F_p) = p - 1$, condition (c)) and the nonsplit norm-one
torus ($\#T_2(\F_p) = p + 1$, condition (d)). In the vocabulary of the
generalized-Carmichael literature, candidates satisfying (c)+(d) are
exactly the \emph{Williams numbers} \cite{Williams77,Echi07}: no example
is known, none exists below $2^{64}$ \cite{McIntoshMitra} (nor below the
Carmichael tables to $10^{18}$ \cite{Pinch}), and
nonemptiness of the class is open --- facts consistent with, and explained
by, the measurements below.

Two elementary lemmas shape everything downstream.

\begin{lemma}[side-coprimality]\label{lem:sides}
If $n$ satisfies (c) and (d) then $\gcd(p_i - 1, p_j + 1) \mid 2$ for all
$i, j$; the odd primes dividing $\prod_i(p_i - 1)$ and those dividing
$\prod_i(p_i + 1)$ form disjoint sets.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
An odd prime $q$ dividing $p_i - 1$ and $p_j + 1$ divides both $n - 1$ and
$n + 1$, hence $2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[twin-smooth dictionary]\label{lem:dict}
Let $p = 2m + 1$. Conditions (b) and the smoothness structure of
$p \mp 1$ hold iff $m \equiv 1 \pmod{40}$, $(m, m+1)$ is a pair of
consecutive smooth numbers (a \emph{twin-smooth} pair) with the odd part
of $m$ supported on one side $Q_-$ and the odd part of $m + 1$ on the
other side $Q_+$, and $p$ is prime.
\end{lemma}

Twin-smooth pairs with $2m+1$ prime are precisely the parameters sought by
the isogeny-based schemes B-SIDH and SQIsign, so their generation is an
industrial literature \cite{CMN21,BCCEMNS23,BHLNV,MSW} whose datasets we
use throughout. Fixing finite disjoint odd prime sets $Q_-, Q_+$ (with
$5 \notin Q_- \cup Q_+$), the pool is
\[
\cP = \bigl\{\, p = 2m+1 \ \text{prime} : m \equiv 1 \!\!\!\pmod{40},\
m \ \text{odd-}Q_-\text{-smooth},\ \tfrac{m+1}{2}\
\text{odd-}Q_+\text{-smooth} \bigr\},
\]
and with $\Lambda_- = 2\lcm\{p - 1\}$, $\Lambda_+ = 4\lcm\{p + 1\}$ over
$\cP$:

\begin{proposition}[reduction to a two-sided subset product]
\label{prop:reduction}
If $S \subseteq \cP$ with $|S|$ odd, $|S| \ge 3$, and
$n = \prod_{p \in S} p$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:target}
n \equiv 1 \pmod{\Lambda_-}, \qquad n \equiv -1 \pmod{\Lambda_+},
\end{equation}
then $n$ is a counterexample to Conjecture~\ref{conj:agrawal}. The targets
in \eqref{eq:target} are consistent because
$\gcd(\Lambda_-, \Lambda_+) = 2$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:sides}).
\end{proposition}

Taking discrete logarithms in each cyclic component (Pohlig--Hellman is
cheap: all component orders are smooth by design), \eqref{eq:target} is a
$0/1$ subset-sum over $G = (\Z/\Lambda_-)^\times \times
(\Z/\Lambda_+)^\times \cong \bigoplus_i \Z/m_i$, plus one $\Z/2$ parity
coordinate. Pigeonhole solvability needs $|\cP| \gtrsim \log_2|G|$ with
slack for the solver. The pipeline is therefore \textbf{harvest} ($\cP$,
with $|\cP| / \log_2|G|$ comfortably $> 1$) then \textbf{solve} (find
$S$). The next two sections treat the stages in turn; all quantitative
claims are measurements, traceable per Appendix~\ref{app:repro}.

\section{Supply is solved}\label{sec:supply}

\subsection{Mining fixed corpora fails: the $0.06$ invariant}

The obvious approach is to mine existing twin-smooth datasets: filter by
Lemma~\ref{lem:dict}, then choose the side partition $Q_- \sqcup Q_+$ that
retains the most elements (an element survives iff its $m$-support and
$(m+1)$-support land on opposite sides). Measured across every scale
available to us --- including the \emph{complete} $B = 547$ corpus of
$82{,}026{,}426$ pairs (courtesy of M.~Naehrig), which yields $73{,}163$
pool candidates after the congruence and primality filters --- the best
partition found by element-greedy search and by direct partition-space
local search keeps
\[
\textbf{$0.060$--$0.083$ elements per demand bit}
\]
(Table~\ref{tab:invariant}), where demand is $\log_2$ of the group order
the survivors generate. Feasibility needs ratio $\gtrsim 1$--$3$: the
shortfall is a factor $16$--$25$, stable across two orders of magnitude in
$B$-range and three in pool size, and insensitive to corpus completeness
and to the optimizer used. Mechanism: a partition-agnostic twin pays
$\approx 2$ fresh primes $\times$ $7$--$7.5$ bits of new demand per kept
element. Dataset mining is dead as the primary route.

As calibration infrastructure we implemented the
Conrey--Holmstr\"om--McLaughlin closure \cite{CHM13,BCCEMNS23} in C; it
reproduces the exact complete twin-smooth sets to $99.2$--$99.7\%$ with
zero false positives and exactly matching largest elements ($B = 100$:
$13{,}333$ of $13{,}374$; $B = 200$: $346{,}110$ against the true
$348{,}840$ --- the originally published count $346{,}192$ was itself
$0.8\%$ short), anchoring the supply curve at $N(B) \sim B^{\alpha}$ with
fitted $\alpha \approx 4.7$--$5.4$.

\subsection{Partition-first harvesting: gates G1 and G2 passed}

The invariant is a global combinatorial tax, and it is bypassed by fixing
the partition \emph{first}: choose $Q_-, Q_+$, enumerate candidates $m$ as
products of $j$ primes of $Q_-$ (no smoothness luck needed on that side),
and test only $(m+1)/2$ for $Q_+$-smoothness and $2m+1$ for primality.
Every hit is partition-compatible by construction; the cost moves into
per-candidate yield, a local Dickman-type probability. Measured ratios of
projected pool to demand bits, at $8$--$20$M samples per cell
(Appendix~\ref{app:tables}, Table~\ref{tab:a2}): $13.8$ at the frozen
production spec ($Q_+ =$ odd primes $\le 1259$ except $5$, $Q_- \subset
(1259, 12590]$, $j = 4$; pool $\approx 2.5 \times 10^5$ of mean $50$-bit
elements against $\approx 18{,}000$ demand bits) up to $209$ at the widest
measured cell --- a factor $200$--$700$ above the mining invariant, and
$5.5$--$84\times$ the go/no-go gate of $2.5$. The underlying yield model
was validated out-of-sample against the complete $B = 547$ corpus on four
asymmetric splits: predicted/actual $0.71, 0.70, 0.41, 1.04$ (geometric
mean $0.68$) --- \emph{conservative, never optimistic}. A solver-aware
co-design variant (both factor bases restricted to primes $q$ with
$(q-1)$'s odd part $100$-smooth) keeps ratio $7.7$ while shrinking the
solver group's odd-prime support from $\{336$ primes $\le 6269\}$ to
$\{24$ primes $\le 97\}$.

Supply, in short, is abundant and over-provisioned. The production harvest
was intentionally never launched, because the binding constraint turned
out to be downstream.

\section{The solver obstruction}\label{sec:solver}

\subsection{The easy part and the generic frontier}

$G$ decomposes into cyclic prime-power components. Its $2$-part is
$\mathrm{GF}(2)$-linear (including the parity coordinate) and solves by
exact Gaussian elimination at full scale: $4{,}586$ components in $12$
seconds at the frozen spec. But the $2$-part carries only ${\sim}16\%$ of
the demand bits; the remaining ${\sim}84\%$ ($\approx 15{,}000$ bits at
the frozen spec) lies in odd-prime components with primes up to $6269$
(up to $97$ after co-design). On that odd part, generic methods hit a hard
frontier, measured on faithful synthetic instances with planted solutions:
simulated annealing inside the $\mathrm{GF}(2)$ solution family stalls at
${\ge}19$ odd components; exact meet-in-the-middle solves $N = 24$,
$D = 11$ instantly and $N = 40$, $D = 15$ in $11$ seconds but is
exponential in $N$, capping near $40$ pool elements and ${\sim}15$ odd
components --- $2$--$3$ orders of magnitude below the thousands of
components and ${\sim}24{,}000$ odd bits required.

\subsection{The scalable family and what it needs}

Subset products of exactly this shape are solved at astonishing scale in
the Carmichael-construction literature: L\"oh--Niebuhr \cite{LN} and
especially Alford--Grantham--Hayman--Shallue \cite{AGHS}, who built a
Carmichael number with $10{,}333{,}229{,}505$ prime factors via a
subexponential $\omega$-guided subset-product reduction over
$(\Z/\Lambda)^\times$ --- including the full odd part, with no
small-odd-part requirement. The method's engine is structural: it needs
pool elements that are the identity on \emph{many} CRT components ---
the ``$p - 1 \mid \Lambda$'' divisor structure, identity-density
$\rho \approx 0.3$--$0.5$ in AGHS pools.

Our harvestable pools do not have this structure. A partition-first pool
prime $p = 2m + 1$ with $m$ a product of $j \approx 4$ medium primes is
the identity on only ${\sim}j$ of the components:
$\rho \approx 0.003$--$0.008$, two orders of magnitude below AGHS.

\subsection{The two-part density gap}

Both halves of the remaining question were measured.

\emph{High density is unharvestable (N1).} The divisor-paradigm harvest
that would supply AGHS-grade density --- fix highly composite
$\Lambda_-, \Lambda_+$ and demand $p - 1 \mid \Lambda_-$,
$p + 1 \mid \Lambda_+$ --- collapses under the double condition: across
all feasible scales the pool is $0$--$2$ primes
(Table~\ref{tab:n1}). The root cause is Lemma~\ref{lem:sides} made
quantitative: the double condition is the conjunction of two rare events
on \emph{disjoint} fixed moduli, where AGHS needs only the single
one-sided event $p - 1 \mid \Lambda$.

\emph{Conclusion of the measurement campaign.} Under the double
Carmichael + Lucas--Carmichael condition one can have a harvestable pool
\emph{or} AGHS-grade identity density, \textbf{not both}: high-density

known scalable family (A10). That two-part, quantified gap is the core
negative result of this paper.

\section{The obstruction is structural}\label{sec:structural}

The measurements of \S\ref{sec:solver} concern $r = 5$ and the specific
section $X - 1$. This section shows none of those choices can be varied
away. The results are proved in the companion arithmetic-geometry document
of this repository (\texttt{doc/arith-geom.tex}, v1.0); we state them with
proofs sketched. One ingredient is conditional and we name it once, here:
\emph{generic sharpness} --- the proved support bounds on the controlling
orders are \emph{attained} --- an Artin-type equidistribution hypothesis,
empirically supported (zero exceptions across $196$ sampled large primes in
three independent verification campaigns) and a \emph{theorem} in the
function-field mirror by Weil's RH for curves. The restriction to
Frobenius-matching mechanisms (\S\ref{sec:scope}, item 2) is a stated
scope, not a hypothesis; the two should not be conflated.

\subsection{The stabilizer-index theorem}

Modulo $p \mid n$, the mechanism's cost is governed by the multiplicative
order of the test section in the component algebra, and that order
collapses exactly when a Galois or CM symmetry fixes the section up to a
root-of-unity cofactor. For $X - 1$, whose zero $\{1\}$ is Galois-stable,
inversion gives the exact identity $(X-1)^{p^{d/2}-1} = -X^{-1}$ for even
$d = \ord_r(p)$ (V\'a\v{n}a's $\rho_5(p) \mid 10(p^2-1)$ \cite{Vana09} is
the case $d = 4$). For $X + 2$, whose zero $-2$ has trivial stabilizer, no
collapse exists at any depth: $(\zeta^\gamma + 2)/(\zeta + 2)$ is not even
a unit of $\Z[\zeta_r]$ for $\gamma \ne 1$ (disjoint ideal supports,
$N(\zeta - 1) = r$ versus $N(\zeta + 2) = (2^r + 1)/3$). For the
$x$-coordinate on a CM elliptic curve, the CM automorphisms give exact
collapses such as $x([i]S) = -x(S)$ on $y^2 = x^3 - x$. All cases are one
statement:

\begin{theorem}[stabilizer-index floor; \emph{proved}, sharpness generic]
\label{thm:stabindex}
For a one-section Frobenius test on a one-dimensional group with scalar
Frobenius action $\lambda$ in a cyclic group and unit-stabilizer $U$ (the
subgroup acting on the section by root-of-unity cofactors), the smoothness
burden a Frobenius-matching forgery must pay at each pool prime is the
index
\[
k' \;=\; [\langle\lambda\rangle : \langle\lambda\rangle \cap U],
\]
realized as simultaneous smoothness of the cyclotomic torus orders
$\Phi_e(p)$ for $e \mid k'$ --- total torus dimension $k'$. Every credible
exception clause equals $U$, because $U$-cells are reachable by one-sided
AGP-type constructions (\S\ref{sec:signsplit}); a valid counterexample
therefore forces $\lambda \notin U$, hence \textbf{burden $k' \ge 2$}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
With $e = k'$ minimal such that $\lambda^e \in U$, the cofactor identity
$f^{\,p^{k'}} = \zeta f$ ($\zeta$ a root of unity of small order) bounds
the order's support by the levels $e \mid k'$ --- unconditionally, by the
exact identities above. For $e < k'$ the cofactor $f \circ [\lambda^e]/f$
is a non-torsion unit (cyclotomic, or elliptic with disjoint divisor
support), so no identity collapses further; generic sharpness then places
the full levels $e \mid k'$ in the order. Validity excludes
$\lambda \in U$ since $U$-cells pass only inside the exception clause.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Every AKS modulus forces the double condition}

\begin{corollary}[torus dimension count]\label{cor:dimcount}
Take the section $X - 1$: $U = \{\pm 1\}$, so the burden at
$p \mid n$ is $d_p' = d_p/2$ ($d_p$ even) or $d_p$ ($d_p$ odd), where
$d_p = \ord_r(p)$, and validity ($\ord_r(n) > 2$, which forces
$d_p \ge 3$) gives burden $\ge 2$ for \emph{every} odd prime modulus $r$.
The minimum $2$ is attained exactly at $d_p = 4$, levels $\{1, 2\}$:
$p - 1$ and $p + 1$ smooth --- the double condition --- realizable iff
$4 \mid r - 1$. For $r \equiv 3 \pmod 4$ the burden is $\ge 3$, i.e.\
smooth values of $\Phi_e(p) \sim p^{\varphi(e)}$ with $\varphi(e) \ge 2$
--- the quantities whose non-smoothness underpins torus-based cryptography
\cite{RS}.
\end{corollary}

The resulting burden formula, $\min_{d \mid r-1,\, d \ge 3}(d/2$ if even
else $d)$, reproduces an independent empirical scan over eleven moduli
$r \le 41$ exactly (value and minimizing $d$), and the collapse identity,
exact orders and sharpness behaviour were verified on $67$ sample primes
--- sampled and implemented independently of that scan (different ranges
and residue-class selection; exact orders by polynomial arithmetic).
\textbf{``Choose a friendlier $r$'' is closed structurally}: every
$r \equiv 1 \pmod 4$ reproduces the $r = 5$ economics identically, and
every other $r$ is strictly worse.

\subsection{Popovych's variant is strictly harder}

\begin{corollary}[Popovych burden]\label{cor:pop}
Take the pair $\{X - 1, X + 2\}$ \cite{Popovych09}: the second section has
$U = \{1\}$, so the pair pays full depth $d_p$ at every parity, and
\[
\mathrm{burden}_{\mathrm{Pop}}(r) = \min\{d : d \mid r - 1,\ d \ge 3\}
\;\ge\; 3 \quad \text{for every } r, \qquad = 4 \text{ at } r = 5,
\]
i.e.\ at Popovych's own modulus the pool primes would need $p - 1$,
$p + 1$ \emph{and} $p^2 + 1$ simultaneously smooth.
\end{corollary}

At the frozen spec the extra ${\approx}102$-bit quadratic level is a pure
Dickman tax $\rho(102/\log_2 B)$: $3.9\times10^{-11}$ over the solver base
$B = 1259$, still $1.8\times10^{-7}$ over the enumerated base
$B' = 12590$ --- emptying the measured $2.5\times10^5$ pool to below one
element; the solver-side group only grows. To our knowledge this is the
first quantified evidence in either direction on Popovych's conjecture
beyond exhaustive search; a self-contained treatment is the companion
note \texttt{doc/popovych-note.tex}. The constant $2$ is immaterial: for
any integer $c \notin \{0, \pm 1\}$ the same trivial-stabilizer argument
gives the identical jump, while $c = \pm 1$ restores the collapse --- the
zero $-c$ must avoid the rational torsion $\{\pm 1\}$ of $\mathbb G_m$,
and the innocent-looking $c = +1$ fails doubly ($\zeta + 1$ is
multiplicatively dependent on the $\zeta - 1$ orbit, so $\{X-1, X+1\}$
adds no burden at all); $c = 2$ is simply the smallest valid choice
(companion note, final remark).

\subsection{The CM-elliptic axis is closed}

A canonical elliptic analogue of the substitution $X \mapsto X^n$ requires
CM: a canonical commuting family of Frobenius lifts would make each
$\psi_p$ a degree-$p$ endomorphism, and an endomorphism of non-square
degree already forces $\mathrm{End}(E) \supsetneq \Z$; conversely for CM
curves the canonical family exists \cite{BorgerGurney}. There Frobenius
acts on an $r$-eigenline by a scalar $\lambda$ and
Theorem~\ref{thm:stabindex} applies with $U$ the image of the CM units:

\begin{corollary}[elliptic closure]\label{cor:elliptic}
On the CM curves the apparent burden-$1$ cells ($\lambda$ equal to a CM
unit, e.g.\ $\lambda \equiv \pm i$, where the section order collapses to
the split torus alone) lie exactly inside the exception clause --- the
elliptic mirror of the rigid cells of \S\ref{sec:signsplit} --- and the
valid floor returns to $2$, attained on levels $\{1, 2\}$: \emph{$p - 1$
and $p + 1$ smooth, the same twin-smooth demand}, with unit-twisted mixed
targets reproducing the sign split. The N1/A10 obstruction of
\S\ref{sec:solver} therefore transfers verbatim.
\end{corollary}

Measured on $y^2 = x^3 - x$: valid-cell floors $3$ at $r = 13$ and $2$ at
$r = 17$, matching the index formula, with the elliptic burden $\ge$ the
torus burden at all eleven moduli tested (and the test degenerate at
$r = 5$, where every cell is exceptional). Supersingular cells have
$\varphi^2 = [-p]$ and reduce to the torus analysis. The elliptic axis
offers no single-rare-event escape.

\subsection{The sign split, not the depth, is the wall}\label{sec:signsplit}

The generalized-Carmichael record explains \emph{which} cells the known
constructive machinery can reach, and confirms the theorem's shape
empirically. Carmichael numbers occupy the cell (level $\{1\}$, Frobenius
power $0$) and are infinite \cite{AGP}; Lucas--Carmichael numbers occupy
$(\{2\}, 1)$ and are infinite \cite{Wright18} (via the elliptic Korselt
criteria of \cite{Silverman12}); the L--P targets occupy
$(\{1,2\}, (0,1))$ --- Williams numbers --- empty to $2^{64}$
\cite{McIntoshMitra}; yet Howe's \emph{rigid order-$2$ Carmichael number}
$443{,}372{,}888{,}629{,}441 = 17\cdot31\cdot41\cdot43\cdot89\cdot97
\cdot167\cdot331$ \cite{Howe00} occupies the \emph{same level set} with
all-even powers ($p^2 - 1 \mid n - 1$; re-verified by us) and exists at
$4.4\times10^{14}$. Depth-$2$ impersonation is constructible; what is not
is the \emph{sign split}: the Williams powers $(0, 1)$ force target $+1$
on the $p-1$ side and $-1$ on the $p+1$ side, which conflict on any
shared prime power and so force the coprime side split of
Lemma~\ref{lem:sides} --- exactly the structure whose harvest collapse is
Table~\ref{tab:n1} and whose solver gap is Table~\ref{tab:a10}. Rigid
cells, by contrast, are one-sided AGP-type problems (single target $1$,
no coprimality demands), which is why the AGP machine reaches them --- and
why the exception clause of any credible conjecture in this family must
contain the stabilizer cells, as Theorem~\ref{thm:stabindex} asserts.
Validity ($n \not\equiv \pm1 \bmod r$) pins the Agrawal construction to
the sign-split cell: a rigid-cell $n$ can pass \eqref{eq:aks} only with
$n \equiv 1 \pmod r$, inside the exception clause --- and indeed Howe's
$n \equiv 1 \pmod 5$ does not even pass $T(-1, n, 5)$ (verified: check H4
of \texttt{code/ag3\_hierarchy\_check.py}), rigidity controlling
$p^2 - 1$ but not the residual $\mu_{2r}$-torsion that the mod-$80$
conditions exist to fix.

\section{Scope, caveats, and what remains open}\label{sec:scope}

This section is the boundary of the result, not throat-clearing.

\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{Not a disproof.} Conjecture~\ref{conj:agrawal} remains open
and believed false; nothing here bears on its truth. Likewise
\S\ref{sec:structural}.3 is evidence about the known \emph{mechanism}
against Popovych's variant, not evidence that the variant is true.
\item \textbf{Mechanism scope.} All structural results quantify
Frobenius-matching constructions ($n \equiv p^{j} \bmod
\lcm(r, \rho_r(p))$ per prime). Every published counterexample approach
\cite{LPremarks,Popovych09,HD21} is an instance; ``accidental''
solutions of the component equations outside this family are not
excluded, and no way to construct them is known.
\item \textbf{Generic sharpness.} The dimension-count and
stabilizer-index theorems bound the support of the control orders
unconditionally (exact collapse identities) but their \emph{attainment}
is an Artin-type genericity statement: a harvest could in principle fish
in the thin set of primes whose section order collapses further
(heuristic density ${\sim}1/\ell$ per avoided large prime; empirically
$0$ exceptions across $196$ sampled large primes in the three
verification campaigns). That set is far
thinner than the twin-smooth pool itself, so the escape is uneconomical,
but it is not unconditionally excluded.
\item \textbf{Finite-scale caveats on N1/A10.} The density-gap
measurements used squarefree divisor harvests and uniform small-prime
component models at finite scale; prime-power moduli and enormous scales
are untested, though the density trend and the two-rare-events argument
point the same way, and the A10 reach law's logarithmic pool-dependence
makes ``outgrow it'' quantitatively hopeless.
\item \textbf{What remains genuinely open.} (i)~A fundamentally new
solver paradigm for $0/1$ subset-products in ${\sim}24{,}000$-bit
smooth-exponent groups with dense vectors and only linear pool/dimension
ratio --- posed precisely, with a regression harness, in the repository's
option-(b) brief. The results of \S\ref{sec:structural} \emph{sharpen
rather than kill} this direction: a breakthrough there would unblock the
torus route only, the Popovych and elliptic variants remaining strictly
harder.
(ii)~The admissible-local-cells question (which residue cells mod $2^k$
and mod $r$ support the mechanism --- the boost polynomials $2x^n - 1$
are excluded by a mod-$16$ obstruction, but other side-separating
families are not ruled out). (iii)~The existence of Williams numbers at
all, open independently of this program \cite{McIntoshMitra}.
(iv)~Unconditional versions of the sharpness statements --- which, we
note, are theorems in the function-field mirror by Weil's RH for curves,
locating the residual difficulty over $\Z$ in the
unconditional-equidistribution gap.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Reusable contributions and conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

Beyond the negative result, the campaign leaves usable artifacts: a
validated C implementation of the CHM closure reproducing complete
twin-smooth sets to $99.2$--$99.7\%$ with exact extremes; a calibrated,
conservative yield model for partition-first smooth-pair harvesting; an
exact $\mathrm{GF}(2)$ solver for the $2$-part of Carmichael-type subset
products; the Wagner density-reach methodology as a \emph{diagnostic} ---
given any proposed Carmichael-type construction, measure its pool's
identity-density and read off the reach --- and the impersonation-cell
vocabulary tying this problem to the Williams/Howe literature, with the
sign-split criterion predicting which cells the AGP machine can reach.
All code and data are committed alongside this paper
(Appendix~\ref{app:repro}).

Two constructive reframings deserve emphasis. First, the stabilizer index
of Theorem~\ref{thm:stabindex} is a \emph{design metric} for AKS-style
tests: for any proposed section (a polynomial section of $\mu_r$, a CM
$x$-coordinate) and any modulus, it computes the smoothness burden a
Frobenius-matching forgery must pay --- in effect a security parameter,
maximized by choosing sections with small unit-stabilizer and moduli whose
$(r-1)$-divisor structure keeps the index high (Table~\ref{tab:stab});
Popovych's pair, read this way, is a deliberate stabilizer reduction from
$\{\pm1\}$ to $\{1\}$. Second, over $\F_q(T)$ (the Carlitz-module mirror)
the sharpness hypothesis is a theorem by Weil's RH for curves, so the
statement \emph{``no Frobenius-matching forgery beats the
stabilizer-index floor''} holds unconditionally there --- a positive
theorem that isolates the residual difficulty of the problem over $\Z$ in
the unconditional-equidistribution gap.

The conclusion in one paragraph: the Lenstra--Pomerance route to an
Agrawal counterexample is a two-stage pipeline whose supply stage we
solved and whose solver stage is blocked by a measured identity-density
gap --- high-density pools are unharvestable under the double condition,
low-density pools are unsolvable by every known scalable family --- and
the blockage is structural: the double condition is the unique
minimum-burden cell for every AKS modulus, the sign split that defines it
is precisely what separates the empty Williams cell from the constructible
rigid cells, Popovych's variant only raises the burden, and the
CM-elliptic analogue obeys the same stabilizer-index floor. A
counterexample to Agrawal's conjecture, if one is ever exhibited, will
require either a genuinely new subset-product solver for the low-density
regime or a mechanism outside the Frobenius-matching family.
